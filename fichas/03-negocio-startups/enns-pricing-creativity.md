# Blair Enns — *Pricing Creativity* (2018)

> Ficha de lectura: mapa para ir al libro, no sustituto. Citas en capítulos (EPUB sin paginación).

## Tesis en tres líneas

1. Value-based pricing (tarificar por el resultado deseado del cliente, no por horas invertidas) produce 100–300% más ingresos en el mismo trabajo que cost-based pricing.

2. El cliente nunca elige entre opciones sobre valor absoluto sino sobre *valor relativo*: necesita 3 opciones con precios diferentes, anclados alto, para comparar y decidir con confianza.

3. Los obstáculos reales no son de mercado sino internos: mentalidad de escasez, baja autoestima, confusión entre igualdad y justicia, e ideas disfuncionales sobre dinero y tiempo son lo que impide cobrar lo que vales.

## Por capítulo

### Cap. 1 · Introduction: What Price for a Logo? (caps. 1–2)

- Nike pagó \$35 (1971) por el swoosh; Pepsi pagó \$1M (2008) por rediseño. No es calidad (no es 5,000x mejor), ni tiempo invertido.
- El riesgo es la variable oculta: Pepsi pagaba por mitigación del riesgo de que el rediseño fallara (Tropicana -\$30M en 2 meses en precedente reciente).
- Valor es construcción subjetiva: no hay "precio correcto" universal. Adam Smith (labor theory, ~100 años) vs. teoría subjetiva (hoy, aceptada). Cliente valúa por: ingresos, costos, y contribuciones emocionales (prestigio, riesgo, paz mental).
- Fairness es un sentimiento, no lógica: cliente se siente justa la transacción si estaría dispuesto a hacerla de nuevo. No es problema nuestro si su sentimiento difiere del nuestro.
- "Double thank-you": momento en closing cuando ambas partes dicen gracias genuinamente. Esa es la marca de agua de pricing justo.

**Cita ancla** (cap. 1): «Fairness is simply the purchaser's feeling that the transaction and price paid were positive enough that they would gladly do it again» (Enns)

### Cap. 2 · Price Like a Marketer (cap. 2)

- Dos culturas irreconciliables: Producer (eficiencia, timesheets, "price taker") vs. Marketer (innovación, valor, "price maker").
- No puedes tener ambas simultáneamente: innovación requiere tiempo y libertad para fallar; eficiencia requiere lo opuesto.
- Ratios como 60/45/20 (utilización/payroll/ganancia) son poderosos hasta ~\$200k AGI/FTE; después, limitan. El timesheet es "ultimate efficiency measure or ultimate innovation killer".
- La presión de commodización viene de tres frentes: cliente (presupuestos históricos), mercado (competencia), y mente propia (creencias limitantes).
- Decisión: ¿Quieres ser commodity producer o valued expert? No hay término medio sin commoditización.

**Cita ancla** (cap. 2): «Broadly speaking, producers pursue efficiencies while marketers pursue innovation» (Enns)

### Cap. 3 · Inputs, Outputs, or Value: What Are You Selling? (caps. 3–4)

- Tres modelos de pricing: (a) inputs/tiempo (\$75k T&M), (b) outputs/deliverables (\$90k fixed price, cliente toma certainty premium), (c) value/resultado (\$200k por \$1M ganancia).
- AGI/FTE (\$160k threshold en 2017) revela estrategia: hay brecha. \$160–200k = firmas eficientes vendiendo inputs; \$250k+ = value-based.
- Value-based pricing es "harder" en práctica: requiere math complejo, conversaciones difíciles, accounting desafiante. 29 de 30 owners saben teoría pero pocas aplican.
- Las firmas que venden value tienen 2–3 clientes tier 1, 4–10 tier 2, "gnats" descartables (Pareto).
- Crecer \$1m → \$2m no es duplicar clientes (10→20) sino aumentar engagement size (\$100k → \$200k).

**Cita ancla** (cap. 3): «In practice, it is a guide to financial mediocrity» (Nagle & Holden)

### Cap. 4 · The Many Ways in Which You Deliver Value (cap. 4)

- Airlines cambian 5M de precios al día. Passenger paga 20x diferente por mismo vuelo pero ambos entienden que compran distintas "formas de valor": timing, leg room, lounge, loyalty points, prestige.
- Para design firms: diseño, creatividad, prestige, speed, research, testing, management, reporting, acceso a principals, garantías, estándares, términos financieros, etc.
- Bundling es clave: no unbundle a la carte (commodifica). Móviles usan "make it easy to understand, difficult to compare"; cambian planes frecuentemente.
- Capacidad = ~10–12 relaciones cliente activas simultáneamente (Pareto universal). Si tuvieras que soltar cliente para tomar uno nuevo, serías más selectivo.
- Warren Buffett analogy: limita "punches" (inversiones) → fuerzas decisiones mejores. Restricción = mejor outcome.

**Cita ancla** (cap. 4): «you'd be forced to load up on what you'd really thought about. So you'd do so much better» (Warren Buffett on limits)

### Cap. 5 · Rule #1: Price the Client, Not the Job (caps. 5–6)

- Price discrimination = algunos dispuestos a pagar más, deben hacerlo. No manipulación; es "willingness to pay" (Hermann Simon). Logo John Doe vs. GM: 100x diferencia en riesgo/ingresos.
- Nunca responda "¿Cuánto cobras por X?" sin "para ese cliente en particular". Si ofreces precio sin contexto del cliente, será bajo. Necesitas info para value assessment.
- Publishing prices: error grave para customized firms (violate Rule #1). OK para productized (estandarizado, repetible, infinitamente escalable).
- MLOE (Minimum Level of Engagement) ≈ 10% de AGI anual. Firma \$1m → MLOE \$100k.
- Customized firms no deben tier servicios (SaaS model). Si estás en "middle" entre customized y productized, estás en commoditización.

**Cita ancla** (cap. 5): «To be precise, there is nothing that you do that is inherently valuable *until* you consider the client» (Enns)

### Cap. 6 · Rule #2: Offer Options (caps. 6–7)

- Humanos no perciben valor absoluto; solo relativo. Necesitan comparación. Stick sin contexto: imposible saber largo. Adelson checkerboard: percepción cambia con contexto.
- Williams-Sonoma breadmaker: \$429 model (no se vendía) duplicó ventas del \$279. La opción cara hizo que la del medio se viera barata.
- Extremeness aversion: con 2 opciones (\$1k, \$1.25k) cliente elige X%, Y%. Añadir tercera (\$1.5k) mueve todo a la derecha: más gente elige \$1.25k.
- Decoys no necesitan ser deseables. Opciones menos deseables hacen que las del medio se vean mejor (como restaurant que coloca anchor caro top-right del menú).
- 3 opciones es óptimo. 4 funciona. >4 causa "paradox of choice" (ansiedad). Nunca 1 opción.

**Cita ancla** (cap. 6): «Human beings cannot subjectively perceive absolute values» (Enns)

### Cap. 7 · Rule #3: Anchor High (caps. 7–8)

- Prada \$500 t-shirt: job no es vender t-shirts de \$500 sino hacer que \$275 se vea barato. Restaurantes colocan anchor expensive en top-right menú.
- Kahneman & Tversky UN experiment: grupo con wheel rigged a 10 → 25% promedio. Grupo con 65 → 45%. Anchor casi no necesita relevancia.
- Anchoring & Adjusting: System 1 (rápido) fija anchor; System 2 (deliberado) intenta ajustarlo pero nunca lo hace completamente.
- **Mito refutado**: "Who speaks first loses" is wrong. Better que TÚ fijes anchor alto que cliente fije uno bajo. Anchor = reference point toda la negociación.
- Timing: presenta anchor PRIMERO (priming effect), no último. Win Without Pitching comienza case studies con el más caro.

**Cita ancla** (cap. 7): «It's not the job of the \$500 price tag to sell \$500 t-shirts. It's the job of the \$500 price tag to sell \$275 t-shirts» (Enns)

### Cap. 8 · Rule #4: Say a Price Before You Show a Price (cap. 8)

- Early objections = friends; late objections = enemies. Si cliente no puede pagar, mejor saberlo antes de escribir propuesta (sunk cost bias).
- **Rule of Money**: "Those who do not talk about it do not make it." Necesitas ser cómodo hablando dinero.
- Mito cultural: convención personal (no hablar dinero) ≠ convención comercial (hablar dinero ES competencia profesional). Confundimos las dos.
- Stress viene de no tener conversación de dinero, no de tenerla (Pressfield: "What's hard is sitting down to write").
- Budget objections varían por nivel org: "We can't afford it" en level bajo ≠ verdadero límite. Execs pueden expandir budget si ven ROI.

**Cita ancla** (cap. 8): «Those who do not talk about it do not make it» (Win Without Pitching Rule of Money)

### Cap. 9 · Rule #5: Master the Value Conversation (caps. 9–10)

- 4 conversaciones del sale arc: (1) Probative (demostrar expertise), (2) Qualifying (vet lead), (3) Value (cuantificar valor), (4) Closing (seal deal).
- **The Flip**: momento cuando pasas de vendor a expert. Señales: sideway glance, cliente toma notas, demurra en proceso.
- **Three-year question**: "It's three years from today... you are really happy... What's happened?" Most powerful sales question (Dan Sullivan). Skips "five whys", va directo a want.
- 4-step framework: (1) Commit client a desired future state, (2) Determine metrics of success, (3) Agree on value of success (quantify), (4) Offer pricing guidance (\$X to \$Y).
- Step 4: Anchor high (\$Y) first, dejar que cliente contribuya \$X (low end). Ej. "If guaranteed \$3m profit, what's fair compensation?"—client reveals fair ROI.
- **Critical**: NUNCA hablar de soluciones en value conversation. Solo: Client → Value → Price → Cost → Solutions (en ese orden).

**Cita ancla** (cap. 9): «It's three years from today and you and I are having coffee. You are really happy with the progress you've made» (Three-Year Question)

### Cap. 10 · Rule #6: Limit Unpaid Proposals to One Page (cap. 10)

- Unpaid proposals = poco/nada discovery más allá de qualifying + value conversations. Soluciones obvias, costos claros (rote work o cliente conocido).
- Paid proposals = diagnostic (discovery required). Vende diagnostic como first step; outcomes = findings + recommendations + 1-página proposal.
- Propuesta = palabras que salen de tu boca. Documento de 1 página es solo visual support. Oral proposal: "There are three ways we can help, at three price points."
- Error histórico: incluir background de firma, bios, free diagnostics, términos, fotos. TODO ridículo. Solo 3 columnas (low/mid/high prices).
- Orden correcto: (1) Options + prices en conversación (supported by 1-pager), (2) Agreement in principle, (3) Contract, (4) Signed + check.

**Cita ancla** (cap. 10): «The sales pitch dressed up as a document is merely a crutch designed to do what you should do but would rather not: *ask*» (Enns)

### Cap. 11 · Crafting High-Priced Options (cap. 11)

- Contingency/Performance Pay: riesgoso (abogados lo hacen bien; mayormente falla para creatives). Consultant cobró \$600k contingent en recaudar \$3.1m; tomó años.
- Portfolio balance: mix high/low risk, balanced según tu apetito (Ronald Baker investment portfolio analogy).
- "If money were no object": Ask yourself what you'd do/charge si cliente no tuviera budget constraining. Esa es tu opción 3 anchor.
- Uncertainty discount: factor back in. Si guaranteed-value anchor fue \$1m, ¿cuánto para opción concierge-level cuando-dinero-no-importa?

**Cita candidata**: «You'd be forced to load up on what you'd really thought about» (Warren Buffett)

### Cap. 12 · Framing Low-Priced Options (cap. 12)

- Buyer types: Price buyers (lowest always, expense mindset), Value buyers (ROI math, investment mindset), Poker players (value buyers faking price sensitivity).
- Price buyer signals: The Flip nunca ocurre. No engage con value conversation. Ven preguntas sobre future state como waste of time.
- Poker players: engageable pero rampean price sensitivity al final. Goal = mejor precio pero value-conscious. Bluffean.
- Cuándo hacer business con price buyers: (a) Tienes exceso capacity junior staff, (b) Estructuras opción baja sin otros drivers (= profitable).
- Selling time en blocks: Strip out terms, access, management, reporting. Solo horas. Client 100% upfront. Si maxean horas, cliente compra otro block (tú decides tamaño).

**Cita candidata**: «It's just a principle of mine that I never pay the first price offered without trying to negotiate» (poker player sobre su MO)

### Cap. 13 · Make the Margin in the Middle (cap. 13)

- Home Depot Option: "You can do it. We can help." Client implementa, firma estrategia + guidance. Ej. \$175k workshop (client + firma experts) vs \$5m full implementation.
- Knowledge Transfer: client people en workshop aprenden. Charge premium. Acéptalo si client dispuesto a pagar más ahora para no hirte después.
- Oversight/Quality Control: creative direction, QC, account management para client's implementation.
- Access to Principals: "Open access" a partners = valuable. Enns pricó su acceso a \$5k/mo mínimo.
- Standards/Guides: brand guidelines, wayfinding. Unbundle a opciones más altas = más revenue.
- Reporting: check-ins diarios, meetings semanales, reportes mensuales. Diferentes clientes quieren diferentes tipos.

### Cap. 14 · Do Retainers Right (cap. 14)

- 3 beneficios: predictable revenue, improved resource allocation, improved relationships.
- 3 desventajas: (a) casi siempre una parte unhappy (firm overserving o underserving), (b) tend to be cost-based, (c) push firma toward productized services.
- Problema: divide budget ÷ 12, divide trabajo ÷ 12. Pero trabajo front-loaded o variable. En 6 meses alguien se siente taken advantage of.
- Retainers = cost-based: firma va a manage toward efficiencies conforme progresa. Raro ver high-margin value-priced retainers.
- 2 usos correcto: (a) Strategic guidance (open access) con Alan Weiss 10% discount si 12-meses upfront, (b) Implementation capacity (AOR).
- Error: budget ÷ 12 automático. Principal rechazó \$50k proyecto porque su mínimo retainer era \$10k/mo.

**Cita ancla** (cap. 14): «One party feels taken advantage of by the other» in retainer relationships (Enns)

### Cap. 15 · Lease Instead of Sell (cap. 15)

- Shift a software-as-service: websites/apps hoy = software. Software hoy se paga monthly. Clients prefer lease over purchase.
- Ejemplo: Client no pagaría \$50k website. Pero sí \$3k/mo × 36 meses = \$108k total. Better client experience, more profitable.
- Royalties model: French designer licencia logos/websites a clients; retiene IP; cobra uso. "Almost able to retire on royalties."
- Precedentes: fotografía (traditional licensing), "Power by the Hour" jet engines, floor coverings en leasing.
- Key: Lease = variable cost → fixed cost para client = price certainty premium.

**Cita ancla** (cap. 15): «Remember: *How* you price is the key to increasing your price» (Enns)

### Cap. 16 · Alternative Pricing Models (cap. 16)

- **Pay What You Like**: Executive Shine (Denver airport) shoeshine. Owner/staff motivated → high quality. Clients pagan \$15–20 (vs \$5–6 elsewhere).
- Satisfaction Guarantee (David Maister): right to pay what work worth if not completely satisfied.
- Tip Clause (Ron Baker): client option to pay more if satisfied. **Must plant seed at agreement** (Cialdini reciprocity); otherwise, oportunidad lost.
- **Points-based model**: criticado. Makes money confuso. Master value conversation instead.
- **Sprints** (Agile): 1–2 week units. Cost-based. Jon Lax (Teehan + Lax) says: better way to build pero not necessarily better way to get paid. Fixed phase 1 + milestone incentives after.

### Cap. 17 · Sell Risk Reduction (cap. 17)

- Premium = precio arriba del normal O protección contra riesgo. En insurance, meanings merge. Biggest risk = performance risk ("Can you really do this?").
- Late in sale, clients overweight consequences of failure, underweight positive outcomes. → Good rationale para offer opción priced higher than stated budget.
- **Critical**: Let clients choose their risk level. Don't impose your risk profile.
- Options = risk spectrum: low-risk (guaranteed, highest price) ↔ high-risk (capacity/time, lowest price) ↔ middle = balanced.
- Speak openly: "I have low-risk option at \$100k. Medium-risk at \$65k. High-risk at \$40k. Which level comfortable to you?"

**Cita ancla** (cap. 17): «The more risk you take away from the client, the more she pays in the form of a premium» (Enns)

### Cap. 18 · Nudge, Nudge; Wink, Wink (cap. 18)

- **Cognitive biases** = heuristics, mental shortcuts. Documented, predictable, ethically nudgeable.
- **Anchoring**: leading high price affects final price.
- **Social Influence**: "Most popular" triggers FOMO. Leverage what peers do.
- **Framing Effect**: presentation affects decision.
- **Loss Aversion**: people dislike giving up 2x más de lo que like acquiring. Try-before-you-buy effective.
- **Scarcity Bias**: rare = valuable. "Only 3-4 new clients/year" o "Only 2 slots for open access to CEO."
- **Charm pricing**: end prices in 9 (\$19k vs \$20k). For high-value proposals, no 9 (implies formula).
- **Confirmation Bias**: most powerful. People seek info supporting preconceptions; ignore challenges. <1% population aware own biases (Kegan & Laskow Lahey).

### Cap. 19 · Never Discount (cap. 19)

- Core rule: NO discounts para win new business (especialmente first job). Discount = new price baseline moving forward.
- **First job must be profitable**. Power diminishes over time.
- Discounts = favors para loyal clients únicamente. Plant seed of reciprocity when offering (Cialdini).
- **Document ALL discounts** en estimates/invoices: real price, label "courtesy discount", final.
- Ask client to name discount ("What would be helpful?") en lugar de guess.
- Consider "free" para best clients on worthy projects (Christmas card, charity). Builds loyalty beyond what asked.

**Cita ancla** (cap. 19): «Those who do not talk about it do not make it» (Win Without Pitching Rule of Money)

### Cap. 20 · Alternatives to Cutting Price (cap. 20)

- **Believe**: Salesperson self-esteem > solution belief. Premium price = measure of how different solution is.
- **Offer Terms**: Si affordability (not value objection), offer payment plan. Client \$50k → \$108k via terms (lease model).
- **Take Out Value**: Si client won't meet price, reduce price BUT strip value. Client da up algo to get lower price.
- **Use Guarantees**: "If after phase 1 unhappy, fire us, refund." Instills confidence. Solo condition = principal involvement.
- **Trade for Larger Commitment**: Discount si client commits volume (pero rare true economies of scale en creative).
- **Walk Away**: Preserves integrity, expert positioning, future re-engagement option. "No" = beginning, not end.

**Cita ancla** (cap. 20): «I'm not questioning your value, we just can't afford it» → **Positive No**: «I hear affordability is issue. Let's stretch payment terms. What terms would you need?» (Enns)

### Cap. 21 · Remove Pricing From the Front Lines (cap. 21)

- Car dealer model: salesperson can't accept any price; must "take to manager". Removes authority. Gives dealership negotiating advantage.
- **Invoking Policy**: use policy as lever ("company policy"). Works great. "They don't let me set price."
- Policy = predetermined decision. Never seen anyone override policy vs. preferences.
- Who sets price?: Not artist in love with craft, not account person in love with client, not new biz desperate for win. Ideally not principal (often main price-cutter).
- **Make next price perfect**: After engagement, ask "How would I price this differently now?"

### Cap. 22 · Negotiate Like a Pro (cap. 22)

- **Power formula**: P = db/D (desirability / own desire). Party wanting engagement most = least power.
- **Don't overinvest**: Sunk costs = (time/effort, emotions, money). Shows neediness, hands power to client.
- **Stop when you've won**: If procurement late, you probably won. They'll claim "down to 2-3 firms"—that's lie.
- **Out-wait the waiter**: Test how badly you want it via speed of response. Out-wait them.
- **Decide concessions in advance**: "give-gets" predetermined (what you give, what you get).
- **Neuter final negotiators**: Prep client in closing: "Procurement will call. Price is price. Can I refer to you?" Leverage power at peak.
- **Alternative forms of No**:
  - Positive No: address underlying need, not specific request.
  - Ask How?: "How would I do that?" Outs outrageous requests.
  - Yes, But: accept specific request then list what must give up.
- **Don't negotiate against yourself**: Each concession = first of many. Use trial close: "If we agree to X, do we have deal or something else in way?"
- **Beware the Rabbit**: firm invited to bid just to drive your price down. Don't be the rabbit.
- **Beware the White Knight**: senior person swoops in, gives away margin fought for. Remove from frontlines.

**Cita ancla** (cap. 22): «Your power is greatest in the moment right after you get a verbal agreement. Leverage it to neuter the final negotiators» (Enns)

### Cap. 23 · Begin to Untether From Time (cap. 23)

- **2-step transition away from SELLING time**: (1) Distinguish new vs old clients (reinvent one client at a time; old = old prices, new = value pricing), (2) Value price strategy first (then creative concepts, then initial application).
- **2-step transition away from TRACKING time**: (1) Track time only for those selling time (free strategy people), (2) Measure profit broadly (firm profit, AGI/FTE, quarterly not by job).
- **Two stages of success** (Dan Sullivan): Stage 1 (hard work + say yes), Stage 2 (innovation + say no). Buffett: "really successful people say no to almost everything."
- Innovation = wasteful, requires time to fail. Saying no creates that time.
- **What is Time?** (Einstein): time ≠ property of universe; it's property of observer, human construct. Relativity: time flexes with space. Photon & observer both right about same photon. Like value/fairness: exists only in mind of observer.

**Cita ancla** (cap. 23): «We may have this time thing backward» (Enns, about tracking time-off vs. tracking time)

### Cap. 24 · The Last Obstacle Is You (cap. 24)

- **Mental barriers**:
  - **Low Self-Esteem**: PP = M × A × SE (Pricing Performance = Motivation × Ability × Self-Esteem). Core belief: "I am worthy". All others built on this. Compensate via options (new biz > capacity, pipeline, cash).
  - **Dysfunctional Ideas About Money**: Money triggers survival response (irrational pero hardwired). View as lifeblood in cooperation system (wealth via trade) vs. zero-sum hoarding.
  - **Labor Theory of Value**: Under stress, default back to "value = cost to make". Reality: after validation, success depends on creativity (see opportunity) + risk = innovation. Not hard work.
  - **Conflating Egalitarianism & Fairness** (Russ Roberts): Egalitarianism (equal rights) ≠ Fairness (personal feeling). You can't decide others' fairness; they'll tell you.
  - **Scarcity Mindset**: believe limited $ to go around. See business as zero-sum. Reality: wealth via intangibles (services, ideas, experiences). Growth sustainable & desirable.
- Entrepreneurs see selves as creating wealth by helping. See trade as beneficial. This clears final barrier to pricing success.

**Cita ancla** (cap. 24): «Most of life's external problems are internal problems» (wise friend quoted by Enns)

## Para Daniel

**Acompañamiento**: Rule #5 (Master the Value Conversation) es el corazón de la venta consultiva del diagnóstico RACE. Los 4 pasos (commit a desired state → metrics → value quantification → pricing guidance) son exactamente la estructura que necesita un consultor para ir de "tengo un problema" a "voy a pagar \$X para que lo resuelvan". Es el cerebro de la conversación de 20–30 min que genera el cierre en la misma llamada.

**Canal Levy**: Las cápsulas sobre "cómo NO commoditizarse" (productized vs. customized firms, Rule #1: Price the Client), "cómo manejar objeciones de precio" (Cap. 20: alternatives to cutting price), y "mentalidad de escasez vs. abundancia" (Cap. 24) son gold para consultores hispanohablantes que cobran \$2–5k/mes y quieren escalar a \$10–50k sin duplicar horas.
