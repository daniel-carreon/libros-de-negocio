# Blair Enns — *The Win Without Pitching Manifesto* (2010)

> Ficha de lectura: mapa para ir al libro, no sustituto. Páginas = número de página **del PDF**
> (el que muestra el visor, no el impreso).

## Tesis en tres líneas

1. Las agencias creativas regalan ideas en pitches porque carecen de expertise verdadera y poder en la relación con clientes; especialización es la única forma de diferenciarse.
2. Vender bien = no persuadir sino facilitar cambio mediante diagnosis profesional, conversación (no presentación) y selectivity; el pitch es adición emocional del vendedor, no necesidad del cliente.
3. Cobrar bien por pensar (diagnóstico y estrategia), no por tiempo; selectivity + expertise + rechazo al trabajo sin pago = poder que atrae clientes que respetan el valor y permite reinvertir en mejora continua.

## Por capítulo

### Cap. 1 · We Will Specialize (pp. 7–15)

- Poder en relación comprador-vendedor depende de alternativas disponibles: especializarse reduce competencia percibida
- Posicionamiento = estrategia (elegir focus) + lenguaje (articularlo) + prueba (construir capacidades)
- Beneficios: ventaja en ventas (gana más a menudo) y premium de precio (cobra más, no compite en precio)
- El Difficult Business Decision: creativos evitan elegir focus porque requiere cerrar puertas, pero sin focus no hay poder
- Dinero es el scorecard básico del éxito; especialización genera margen que permite reinversión y fortaleza empresarial

**Cita ancla** (p. 7): «Expertise is the only valid basis for differentiating ourselves from the competition. Not personality. Not process. Not price.»

### Cap. 2 · We Will Replace Presentations With Conversations (pp. 16–25)

- Creativos adictos a adrenalina de presentación (win/lose); esto perpetúa dependencia de pitch
- Roles: performer (auditando talento) vs. practitioner (asesor experto); el segundo negocia desde poder
- Con clientes existentes: Rules of Collaboration (Strategy First, Continuous Reference, Freedom of Execution, Fewer Better Options, Only We Present)
- Con prospectos: Mission = Position as expert; Objective = Determine fit (no es convencer, no es vender)
- Presentación persuade (sway); conversación pesa alternativas (weighing) → conversación baja resistencia de compra

**Cita ancla** (p. 22): «Presenting is a tool of swaying, while conversing is a tool of weighing.»

### Cap. 3 · We Will Diagnose Before We Prescribe (pp. 25–30)

- Prescribir sin diagnosticar = malpractice; en otras profesiones genera responsabilidad legal
- Clientes self-diagnósticados suelen estar equivocados (necesitan Y pero dicen necesitar X), bloqueados por constraints que ven inmutables
- Outside perspective del experto requiere libertad diagnóstica: agencias sin proceso propio se dejan dictar proceso por clientes
- Fábula del Escorpión: clientes exitosos tienen naturaleza de controlar, aunque no siempre es en su interés; firma responsable resiste
- Bad engagements raíz = dejar cliente dictar, aceptar self-diagnosis, convertirse en order-taker

**Cita ancla** (p. 29): «We see him as demanding and difficult. He sees us as irresponsible order-takers not worth the money he is paying.»

### Cap. 4 · We Will Rethink What it Means to Sell (pp. 31–42)

- Miedo a "selling": creativos lo ven como persuasión distasteful; pero selling profesional ≠ persuasión = facilitación de cambio
- Dos salespeople: uno que ve falta de fit y steers away (respectful) vs. uno que presiona (sleazy)
- Selling = change management; tres pasos según lugar en buying cycle:
  - Help the Unaware: thought leadership, educación (no persuasión)
  - Inspire the Interested: portfolio y ejemplos inspiracionales
  - Reassure the Intent: después de decisión, buyer's remorse es real; ofrecer alternativas (guarantees, terms, not proposals)
- Cuatro prioridades: Win Without Pitching → Derail Pitch → Gain Inside Track → Walk Away

**Cita ancla** (p. 34): «To sell is to: Help the unaware, Inspire the interested, Reassure those who have formed intent.»

### Cap. 5 · We Will Do With Words What We Used to Do With Paper (pp. 43–50)

- Propuesta = lo que sale de tu boca; documento = contrato, escrito solo después de acuerdo verbal
- Over-investment en propuesta = crear buying resistance (cliente piensa que necesitas su negocio)
- Razones falsas por las que cliente pide propuesta: para keep hordes at bay, compare, measure value, gain inspiration, stall, shop price
- Si tienes expertise verdadero, cliente mejor posicionado cede façade de RFP
- Propuesta escrita es "tool of swaying" como presentación; conversación es "tool of weighing"
- Para diagnóstico complejo: cobrar por diagnostic phase (como médico por MRI, lawyer por discovery)

**Cita ancla** (p. 50): «Our proposal is indeed the words that come out of our mouths: We propose to do X for you, over Y timeframe, for Z price.»

### Cap. 6 · We Will Be Selective (pp. 51–58)

- Selectivity = característica del experto; builds credibility, reduces buying resistance
- "No is second best answer we can hear" (primero es "yes"); matar deals pronto es eficiencia
- Small number of high-quality clients > many small clients; churn sano e inevitable
- Raise objections first (money, client willingness, geography, etc.) → cliente supera o no; if not → early kill
- Expertise gap: okay aceptar work outside expertise si tienes capacidad, pero NO si implica competir; si cliente lo solicita → decline
- Selectivity deepens con expertise: experto una vez probó poder, nunca vuelve a position del generalist

**Cita ancla** (p. 52): «It is human nature to follow what retreats from us and to back away from what advances.»

### Cap. 7 · We Will Build Expertise Rapidly (pp. 59–65)

- Focus → claim expertise → prove it; announcement atrae atención y competencia, pero es precio de poder
- Three skills: consulting first (problem-seeing), writing second (prove y deepens expertise), artistry third (commodity increasingly)
- Writing = fastest way deepen knowledge, get found, cement position; mandatory para expertos
- Formalized processes = consistency de quality; clients se preguntan "How do I know I'll get their best work?" → proceso define answer
- Training y continuous learning = culture mandatory; algunos no follow → shepherd away
- "The One Who Eddied Out" = principal que tuvo early success sin decisions difíciles, después cuando mercado cambió no supo adaptarse

**Cita ancla** (p. 62): «If we are to be experts we must write.»

### Cap. 8 · We Will Not Solve Problems Before We Are Paid (pp. 66–69)

- Thinking = highest value product; si no lo valúas, cliente no lo valúa
- Línea: probar ability ≠ solving problem; diagnóstico completo es crossing line sin pago
- Free spec creative AND free strategy/diagnosis = free thinking
- Escalation of commitment: private commitment (me lo digo) → shared (te lo digo) → signed → paid; full commitment solo after payment
- Deposit policy: 1/3 a 1/2 de fee total, or entire fee for phase 1; "It is our policy for all new clients"
- Never tentative about deposits; state as policy, not as request

**Cita ancla** (p. 68): «Every client reserves the right to change his mind until he parts with his money.»

### Cap. 9 · We Will Address Issues of Money Early (pp. 70–75)

- Money rule: Those who cannot talk about it, do not make it
- Money stress = stress from not having conversations you know you should have
- Minimum Level of Engagement: ~10% of annual target fee income; use to weed poor fits, escalate tactical to strategic, start money talk early
- "We only work with small number of new clients yearly; clients must spend at or above this level"
- Committed but flexible: mention routinely, reserve right to waive; waiving without mentioning = failure to follow selectivity
- Project work: solo si hay capacity, profitable, no impair future strategic work, no competition

**Cita ancla** (p. 70): «In every culture it is considered a sign of poor business acumen to avoid talking about money in a business setting.»

### Cap. 10 · We Will Refuse to Work at a Loss (pp. 76–81)

- Every project = profit (except carefully selected pro bono); never loss leaders
- Profit margin greatest in diagnose/prescribe phases (thinking), diminishes after (doing); optimize for margin early
- Cutting price = become "price shop" forever con ese cliente; negotiate instead on guarantees (diagnostic phase), terms (payment), holding ground
- Two rules if discount: leave it last (solve all objections first), put it in writing (so client doesn't forget true value)
- Pro bono duality: truecharity gives courage to refuse marginally profitable work; every for-profit = profit, every pro bono = fulfillment
- Early stage: okay trade profit for expertise if honest about it; later = maintain every-for-profit-is-profitable rule

**Cita ancla** (p. 80): «This is the emancipating duality of pro bono work: it is the charity we must do, and it gives us the courage to turn away»

### Cap. 11 · We Will Charge More (pp. 81–86)

- Increase pricing as expertise deepens; smallest invoices = most annoying; charging more creates time to think vs. invoice for changes
- Use pricing as positioning tool; admit "we're likely more expensive than other options"; don't apologize
- Losing business on price occasionally = not charging enough; needing to win on price = not differentiated as expert
- Premium pricing improves client commitment: clients need to feel pain to act on advice; larger clients = greater value derived = should pay more
- Healthy margins fix mistakes, build loyalty; ringing phone test: profitable client = pick up; unprofitable = cringe
- Change order represents most wrong: fired over small tactical invoices, not large strategic; disappears with proper pricing + control
- Sell thinking not time/hours; charges strategic work (diagnose/prescribe) in big round numbers, not hourly
- Reinvestment: profit margins allow R&D, improve people, increase distance from competition

**Cita ancla** (p. 85): «We are equally obliged to give him the strength to do it»

### Cap. 12 · We Will Hold Our Heads High (pp. 87–90)

- Creative professions at crossroads: design as differentiator vs. outputs commoditized; middle disappearing
- Forced choice: expert (premium price, control, respect) or order-taker (pitch, low margin, commoditized)
- Power we wield = power to walk away; free-pitching problem never disappears de mundo, solo disappears from us si we're selective
- Seek respect above money; money follows respect; money validates gifts, gives strength to impact world
- Dreams alone not enough; need business practice skills too; Difficult Business Decision required
- We see what others cannot; can conceive what doesn't exist; at our best, what problem cannot we solve?
- Cause of revolution: not rid world of pitching, but build business that reaches highest heights y impacts world larger than us
- 12 proclamations = steps on path toward vision

**Cita ancla** (p. 89): «we will leave the poor clients to our competitors, and in this way acknowledge that the free-pitching problem is never going away – it is»

## Para Daniel

Este libro es mapa directo para Diagnóstico RACE: "diagnose before prescribe" es eje central, igual que "respectful facilitator" vs. persuasión. Para Acompañamiento: el module de "cómo vender sin pitching" es aplicación directa de las 12 proclamaciones (especialización → selectivity → premium positioning). Canal de Levy puede incluir explainers sobre: (1) por qué la mayoría de agencias regala ideas (power/positioning gap), (2) tres pasos de selling (help/inspire/reassure) para consultant que busca clientes primeros, (3) "Minimum Level of Engagement" como tool de selectivity early en prospecting.
