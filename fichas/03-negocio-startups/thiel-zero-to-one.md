# Peter Thiel — *Zero to One: Notes on Startups, or How to Build the Future* (2014)

> Ficha de lectura: mapa para ir al libro, no sustituto. Páginas = número de página **del PDF**
> (el que muestra el visor, no el impreso).

## Tesis en tres líneas

1. El futuro requiere crear cosas nuevas (0→1) no copiar las existentes (1→n); solo los monopolios conseguidos por solucionar un problema único generan valor duradero.

2. La ideología de la competencia perpetúa la mediocridad: la verdadera creación requiere pensar desde primeros principios, identificar secretos que otros no ven, y construir empresas pequeñas con equipos cohesionados unidos por una misión.

3. El éxito de una startup depende menos de talento individual y más de obtener correctamente siete elementos clave: tecnología (10x mejor), timing, monopolio de nicho, equipo, distribución, durabilidad y el descubrimiento de una oportunidad única que otros ignoran.

## Por capítulo

### Prefacio · Zero to One (pp. 6–7)

- Cada momento en los negocios ocurre una sola vez: el próximo Gates no construirá un SO, el próximo Zuckerberg no creará una red social
- Progreso horizontal (1→n): copiar lo que funciona, más de lo mismo, globalization
- Progreso vertical (0→1): crear algo radicalmente nuevo, lo que nadie ha hecho
- La tecnología permite hacer más con menos, ratcheting up fundamental capabilities
- Los humanos redefinen el plan del mundo mediante innovación, no por instinto
- El libro nace de notas de Blake Masters de un curso en Stanford (2012), condensadas desde una visión empresarial de primeros principios

**Cita ancla** (p. 6): «Technology is miraculous because it allows us to do more with less, ratcheting up our fundamental capabilities to a higher level.»

### Cap. 1 · The Challenge of the Future (pp. 8–13)

- La pregunta de entrevista definitiva: "¿Qué verdad importante crees que pocos acuerdan contigo?"
- Contrarian thinking: verdades impopulares vs. conformidad convencional; el futuro se construye con ideas no convencionales
- Progreso horizontal (globalization): copiar desde mercados existentes, llevar tecnología vieja a nuevos lugares (China copia West)
- Progreso vertical (tecnología): crear cosas completamente nuevas que nadie ha hecho (ej. typewriter→word processor)
- Sin cambio tecnológico, la globalización resulta insostenible (China duplicada = duplicada contaminación)
- Las startups: grupos pequeños unidos por misión capaces de cambiar el mundo, equilibrio entre colaboración y pequeñez
- Pensar desde primeros principios, no fórmulas: los emprendedores exitosos encuentran valor en lugares inesperados

**Cita ancla** (p. 9): «Vertical or intensive progress means doing new things—going from 0 to 1»

### Cap. 2 · Party Like It's 1999 (pp. 14–22)

- Contexto del boom: 1990–1992 recesión global, malaise, miedo a globalización (Perot 20% votos); recovery lento
- Mosaic browser (1993)→Netscape (1994)→IPO (1995, de $28→$174 en 5 meses): nacimiento del boom
- Dot-com mania (Sept 1998–Mar 2000): empresas absurdas competían, no por producto sino por ser "internet"
- PayPal: valuation $500M (WSJ Feb 2000), levantó $100M March 2000 justo antes del crash
- Crash NASDAQ: 5048 (Mar 2000)→3321 (Apr 2000)→1114 (Oct 2002); interpretado como juicio divino
- Post-crash dogmas: pequeños pasos incrementales (evitar visiones grandes), flexibility (no planes), mejorar competencia existente, product over sales
- Verdad opuesta (mejor): arriesgar en lo grande, malo un plan que no tener, evitar competencia, sales matters
- La competencia perfecta = equilibrio = muerte; el bubble de 1999 fue pico de insanidad Y pico de clarity (visión 20-30 años adelante)

**Cita ancla** (p. 21): «It is better to risk boldness than triviality.»

### Cap. 3 · All Happy Companies Are Different (pp. 23–31)

- Pregunta del negocio: "¿Qué empresa valiosa nadie está construyendo?"
- Crear valor ≠ capturar valor; aerolíneas crean $160B de valor pero margen $0.37/pasajero; Google $50B pero 21% ganancia
- Monopolio = empresa "tan buena en lo que hace que ninguna otra ofrece sustituto cercano" (ej. Google en búsqueda)
- Competencia perfecta: firms undifferentiated, venden a precio mercado, sin profits en long-run
- Mentiras asimétricas: monopolistas mienten minimizando poder (fingir competencia), competidores mienten exagerando unicidad
- Empresas competitivas: brutales, márgenes bajos, empleados explotados (restaurantes, Michelin chefs suicidios); monopolios pueden ser éticos
- Creative monopoly: añaden categorías nuevas de abundancia al mundo (Apple iPhone no quitó nada, creó categoría)
- Tolstoy opuesto: todas las familias felices son iguales (= competencia); todas las empresas felices son diferentes (cada una = monopolio por resolver problema único)

**Cita ancla** (p. 31): «All happy companies are different: each one earns a monopoly by solving a unique problem.»

### Cap. 4 · The Ideology of Competition (pp. 32–38)

- Competencia no es deseable inevitable: es ideología perpetuada por sistema educativo (grades, rankings, credenciales)
- Elite students compiten por carreras convencionales (consulting, banking), pierden sueños propios; Thiel casi gana Supreme Court clerkship (habría sido trap)
- Modelos de conflicto: Marx (diferencias generan pelea), Shakespeare (similitud genera pelea)
- Negocio = Shakespeare: Microsoft vs. Google peleaban porque eran idénticos (ambos soñaban dominar mundo), resultado: Apple los desplazó
- Square vs. imitators: batalla sobre formas de lectores (cuadrado→semicírculo→cilindro→triángulo); absurdo strategically
- Personas con Asperger/social ineptness: ventaja en Silicon Valley porque no copian multitud, obsesionados con craft
- Pets.com vs. competidores: guerra tribal por marketless online pet store, $300M desaparecieron
- Ellison vs. Siebel: enemistad personal (truckloads ice cream sandwiches), billboards; ambos perdieron focus
- PayPal vs. X.com: guerra total (100-hour weeks, ingeniero diseñó bomb), mejor merger (fear bubble) que aniquilación
- Lección: if you fight, strike hard and end it quickly; si no, don't fight

**Cita ancla** (p. 35): «Rivalry causes us to overemphasize old opportunities and slavishly copy what has worked.»

### Cap. 5 · Last Mover Advantage (pp. 39–50)

- Valor empresa = suma flujos futuros descontados (cash flows a valor presente)
- Twitter $24B (pierde dinero) > NY Times $2B (gana dinero) porque: Twitter = valor en futuro lejano, Times = monopolio en declive
- 4 características de monopolio: proprietary technology (10x mejor), network effects, economies of scale, branding
- 10x rule: solo order of magnitude mejor = escape competencia (ej. iPad hizo tablets usables desde inusables)
- Amazon: monopolio eBay "PowerSellers" (25% en 3 meses) > intentar mercado masivo
- Network effects: primero mercado pequeño concentrado (Facebook = Harvard first, no Earth); Xanadu fracasó (quería todos simultaneously)
- Scaling: Amazon libros→CDs/videos→general store (nombre = biodiversidad rainforest→biodiversity todo); eBay: Beanie Babies→subastas generales
- No disruption: PayPal expandió mercado pagos, no atacó Visa; win-win > zero-sum
- Last mover > first mover: generar cash flows por décadas/life, domina niche luego expande deliberado

**Cita ancla** (p. 39): «Simply stated, the value of a business today is the sum of all the money it will make.»

### Cap. 6 · You Are Not a Lottery Ticket (pp. 50–68)

- ¿Luck o skill? Serial entrepreneurs (Jobs, Musk, Dorsey: múltiples billion-dollar companies) sugieren skill, pero narrativa luck domina
- 4 vistas del futuro: definite/indefinite × optimism/pessimism
- Indefinite pessimism: Europa 1970s onwards = bureaucratic drift sin plan, reacciona a events, Euro casi colapsa
- Definite pessimism: China = crece rápido copiando West pero sabe insostenible, líderes tienen miedo, ciudadanos ahorran
- Definite optimism: 1600s–1960s = ambición grande, Empire State 1929–31, Golden Gate 1933–37, Interstate 1956–65, Apollo 1961–72
  - John Reber Bay Plan: schoolteacher con visión (2 dams, 20k acres, Army Corps model), Congressional hearings, tomaban en serio
- Indefinite optimism: 1982–hoy = finance > engineering, bankers/lawyers/private equity, opcionalidad sin plan
  - Founders sin visión venden a precio indefinite; Steve Jobs rechaza Yahoo $1B (2006) porque tenía visión definite
  - Biotech: indefinite approach (random molecular screening) vs. definite physics (principios first)
  - Lean startup/MVP: Darwinism empresarial, iteration sin plan, incompatible con 0→1
- Tu rol: pensar definite, actuar definite sobre oportunidades futuras

**Cita ancla** (p. 51): «If you treat the future as something definite, it makes sense to understand it in advance.»

### Cap. 7 · Follow the Money (pp. 68–77)

- Power law (Pareto): 20% gente, 80% riqueza; 20% peapods, 80% peas; todo mundo = extreme inequality
- Venture capital = power law extremo: mejor inversión en fondo > suma rest (Facebook en Founders Fund 2005 > todas otras)
- VCs piensan normal distribution (malo fail, mediocre flat, bueno 2-4x); realidad = power law (uno domina)
- Regla #1 VC: invertir SOLO en empresas que puedan devolver value del ENTIRE FUND
- Regla #2: no hay reglas (porque rule #1 tan restrictiva elimina 99%)
- Instagram: $250K→$1B sale = $78M return (312x) insuficiente para Andreessen (need 19 Instagrams para break-even)
- Startup world = power law: uno better market, uno distribution strategy, tiempos críticos > el resto
- Educación enseña: excellence en todo (flexible portfolio) = falso; mejor: focus obsesivo, especialidad única
- If found startup: 0.01% Google > 100% failed startup; no necessary found si best company exists

**Cita ancla** (p. 71): «The biggest secret in venture capital is that the best investment in a successful fund equals or outperforms the entire rest of the fund combined.»

### Cap. 8 · Secrets (pp. 78–88)

- Contrarian question presupone: secrets still exist (truths about mundo no commonly known)
- Kaczynski (Unabomber): goal-trichotomy: fácil (cualquiera), imposible (Einstein no puede), difícil (satisfaction)
  - Hipsterdom: nostalgia porque mundo moderno = fácil o imposible (no difícil, no satisfacción)
- 4 razones CONTRA buscar secrets:
  1. Incrementalism: escuela enseña tiny steps (cada quien avanza similar pace)
  2. Risk aversion: miedo a estar solo + equivocado (más unbearable que solo + right)
  3. Complacency: élites cómodas no buscan (why work if ya tienes?)
  4. Flatness: global talent pool "si posible, someone smarter already found it"
- Resultado: can't start cult (menos crazy cults, pero perdimos wonder at secrets)
- Mundo sin secrets = imperfect justice, bubble impossibility, pero bubbles existen (1999, 2005, mercados ineficientes)
- Hewlett-Packard: 1990 $9B→2000 $135B (invention decade)→2005 $70B (perdió search for new)
- Andrew Wiles: Fermat's Last Theorem 358 años, creyó secrets, probó 1995 (9 años dedicados)
- Silicon Valley secrets: Airbnb/Uber/Lyft = spare capacity VISIBLE pero no usada antes
- 2 tipos: natural secrets (física undiscovered) vs. people secrets (tabú, forbidden knowledge)

**Cita ancla** (p. 85): «Great companies can be built on open but unsuspected secrets about how the world works.»

### Cap. 9 · Foundations (pp. 89–96)

- "Thiel's law": startup con foundational mistake NO se arregla
- Co-founder choice = crítico (Luke + otro en 1998 startup = terrible match, broke)
- Ownership vs. Possession vs. Control:
  - Ownership: equity legal?
  - Possession: quien opera day-to-day?
  - Control: quien governs?
- DMV ejemplo: citizens = owners (democracy) pero possess = bureaucrats, control ≠ exists, resultado = misaligned mess
- Board size: 3 ideal, máx 5 (público maybe 9), NUNCA gigantic (false facade dictatorship)
- Full-time only: part-time/consultants/remote = fundamentally misaligned, claim value near-term NO help future
- "On the bus or off the bus" (Kesey): binary decision, no middle ground
- CEO pay <$150K early-stage venture (high pay = defend status quo, low pay = fix problems, set ceiling cash)
- Aaron Levie (Box): lived studio no furniture, all saw commitment
- Equity > cash: orienta al futuro, crea commitment long-term (cash = optionality = claim value existing)
- Equity allocation: nunca equal (unfair anyway), nunca disclose (nuclear bomb en office)
- Founding extend: maintain openness a invention (creation vs. stewardship inherited success)

**Cita ancla** (p. 89): «Friends have teasingly nicknamed it Thiel's law: a startup messed up at its foundation cannot be fixed.»

### Cap. 10 · The Mechanics of Mafia (pp. 97–103)

- Ideal culture: cliché perks (beanbags, yoga, dog) sin substance NO funciona
- Company = culture itself, no separate thing
- PayPal Mafia: cohesión extraordinaria, después: Facebook/YouTube/LinkedIn/SpaceX/Palantir/Yelp/Yammer (7 companies >$1B cada)
- No résumé hire: buscamos relaciones durables, gente que quiera trabajar juntos (vs. professional transactional)
- Time = most valuable asset; no gastar con gente que no te gustan (falso en financial terms)
- Recruiting = core competency, never outsource
- ¿Por qué unirse como 20th engineer a startup vs. Google?
  - Bad pitches: stock options worth more, smartest people, hard problems (todos claman esto)
  - Good pitches: misión compelling (PayPal = digital currency), compatible team specifically
- Silicon Valley hoodie/uniform: tribal outside (look same), but unique roles inside (sharp individual distinction)
- One thing per employee: reduce conflict (no competing responsibilities)
  - Max Levchin: early staff as personally similar as possible (shared worldview, nerd, sci-fi readers, obsessed digital currency)
- Cults vs. consultants spectrum:
  - Cults: total dedication, abandon family/outside, belonging, esoteric truths
  - Consultants: no mission, people drop in/out, transactional
  - Best startups: slightly less extreme cults, fanatically RIGHT (not wrong)

**Cita ancla** (p. 102): «The best startups might be considered slightly less extreme kinds of cults.»

### Cap. 11 · If You Build It, Will They Come? (pp. 103–112)

- Sales subestimado (especially Silicon Valley); Hitchhiker's Guide: B Ship (salesmen) landed Earth (A/C pensaba inútiles)
- Distribution = critical, no magic; "Field of Dreams" fallacy: customers won't come just build
- Nerds vs. salesmen: advertising works (on nerds too), pero niegan; sales superficial vs. hard work parecer easy
- Sales hidden job titles: "account executive", "business development", "investment banker", "politician"
- Tom Sawyer grandmaster: convince amigos whitewash fence, luego pagar privilege
- Distribution continuum:
  1. Complex sales ($7M+): months relationships, CEO involved (SpaceX Elon, Palantir Alex 25 days/month); 50-100% YoY growth
  2. Personal sales ($10K-$100K): sales team, establish process; Box 2008 Stanford Sleep Clinic→enterprise-wide
  3. Distribution doldrums ($1K): no good channel (ads too broad, sales too expensive) = hidden bottleneck
  4. Marketing/advertising: low price, mass appeal, no viral; Warby Parker TV ads (CLV $few hundred)
  5. Viral: core functionality invites friends; PayPal $20/customer, 7% daily growth, PowerSellers eBay segment key
- Power law distribution: uno funciona, rest fracasan; no kitchen sink approach
- CLV > CAC siempre
- Selling company too: employees, investors, media

**Cita ancla** (p. 105): «It's better to think of distribution as something essential to the design of your product.»

### Cap. 12 · Man and Machine (pp. 113–121)

- Deep Blue vs. Kasparov, Watson vs. Jennings, self-driving cars: computers outperform humans some tasks
- Fear: machines replace workers; WRONG: computers = complements, not substitutes
- Humans compete jobs/resources; computers NO compete
- Globalization = substitution (workers compete, wants converge: pork, Cristal everywhere)
- Technology = complementarity: humans intentional/decide complex, computers efficient data/fail simple judgment
- Google cat: 10M videos 75% accuracy; 4-year-old 100%; humans/computers categorically different
- Trade with computers: NO (no demand resources: villas Cap Ferrat); gains without competing resources
- Complementary businesses:
  - PayPal fraud: hybrid (software flag suspicious, human judge final); Igor system →first Q profit
  - Palantir: software analyze data, human analyst review, cannot alone
  - LinkedIn: transform recruiters NOT replace, 97% recruiters use LinkedIn
- Computer science bias substitution (machine learning, big data)
  - Machine learning: Netflix/Amazon recommendations, Google Translate patterns
  - "Big data" = usually dumb data; humans interpret, actionable insights
- Strong AI future worry 22nd century, not today; Luddites + futurists both wrong; room in between build better world

**Cita ancla** (p. 116): «We don't trade with computers any more than we trade with livestock or lamps.»

### Cap. 13 · Seeing Green (pp. 122–137)

- 21st century cleantech consensus: smog Beijing, arsenic Bangladesh, hurricanes climate = need solutions
- $50B venture investment → failure: Solyndra, 40 solar manufacturers bankruptcy 2012
- 7 preguntas TODA empresa debe contestar (cleantech failed most):
  1. Engineering: 10x better or incremental? (Solyndra cylindrical = π worse)
  2. Timing: now right? (solar 1954 6%→2000s 25% LINEAR vs. Moore exponential)
  3. Monopoly: big share small market? (MiaSolé: huge market = competition, thin-film 1 of 12+)
  4. People: right team? (non-technical CEOs in suits = red flag for Founders Fund)
  5. Distribution: deliver? (Better Place $800M, 1000 cars, hoops)
  6. Durability: defensible 10-20 yrs? (China undercut, fracking disrupted: 2000 1.7%→2013 34% gas)
  7. Secret: unique opportunity? (conventional truth ≠ secret)
- Social entrepreneurship: "do good doing well" = usually neither; ambiguity social goals
- Tesla 7/7: technology 10x integrated (Consumer Reports highest), timing $465M DOE one-time 2010, monopoly $109K Roadster→Model S, people Elon engineer-salesman, distribution owned stores, durability founder in charge, secret fashion + cleantech = cool brand

**Cita ancla** (p. 137): «Paradoxically, the challenge for the entrepreneurs who will create Energy 2.0 is to think small.»

### Cap. 14 · The Founder's Paradox (pp. 138–153)

- PayPal 6 founders: 4 bomb builders, 5 age 23, 4 overseas-born, 3 escaped communism = unusual
- Normal traits = bell curve; founder traits = inverse normal (extremes both sides simultaneously)
- Contradictions: cash poor + millionaires paper, jerks + charismatic, insiders + outsiders, famous + infamous
- Nature/nurture/exaggeration reinforce cycle
- Richard Branson: naturally entrepreneur (Virgin 16, 22 Records) pero trademark mane cultivated PR; media enthrones king
- Sean Parker: hacker→criminal→Napster 10M users→Facebook president→notoriety→coolest America (JT looks like Sean)
- Lady Gaga: "born this way" pero completely self-manufactured zombie horns
- Mythology: Oedipus insider/outsider, Romulus insider-criminal-founder-king, scapegoats = worshipped gods pre-execution
- Monarchy roots: living god + scapegoat delayed death
- Celebrity "American royalty": Elvis/Michael/Britney rise→fall→immortal (27 Club: Joplin/Morrison/Cobain/Winehouse)
- Howard Hughes: engineer wealthy, planes records, Congressional Medal 1939, crash 1946 → obsessive-compulsive 30 years solitary
- Bill Gates: nerd eyeglasses, 90% OS 2000, DOJ lawsuit forced legal defense, stagnation era
- Steve Jobs: barefoot dropout insider + cult, kicked 1985, return 12 yrs, interim CEO 1997 near-bankrupt, iPod 2001/iPhone 2007/iPad 2010, 2012 most valuable company
- Lesson: individual prominence = can become notoriety anytime; founders valuable bring best from others, not independent
- Not Ayn Rand: no Galt's Gulch, secession impossible
- Greatest danger founder: belief own myth → lose mind; equal danger: lose myth confuse disenchantment wisdom

**Cita ancla** (p. 152): «Founders are important not because they are the only ones whose work has value.»

### Conclusión · Stagnation or Singularity? (pp. 154–158)

- 4 possible futures (Bostrom):
  1. Recurrent collapse: unlikely (knowledge widespread, complete annihilation more probable)
  2. Convergence plateau: most expect (globalization same), unsustainable without tech (competition scarce resources → conflict)
  3. Extinction: conflict+scarcity+no-tech → collapse
  4. Singularity: new tech → much better future (Kurzweil's Moore's law extrapolation confident)
- Reality: choice between nothing or something
- Future won't happen solo; need work today
- Everything singular: universe, planet, country, company, life, moment
- Task: find singular ways create new things 0→1
- Essential: think yourself
- See world anew como ancients → recreate + preserve
