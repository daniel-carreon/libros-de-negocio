# Mark Roberge — *The Sales Acceleration Formula* (2015)

> Ficha de lectura: mapa para ir al libro, no sustituto. Páginas = número de página del PDF.

## Tesis en tres líneas

1. Sales acceleration requiere **cuatro fórmulas predecibles** (hiring, training, management, demand generation) aplicadas con disciplina de ingeniero y métricas cuantificables.
2. En era de comprador empoderado, sales exitoso significa **consultor confiable que entiende buyer's job** (no agresivo; inbound > outbound).
3. **Experimentación disciplinada**, transparencia de datos y cultura de innovación escalable son los levers finales para acelerar de $0 a $100M.

## Por capítulo

### Cap. 1 · Uncovering the Characteristics of a Successful Salesperson (pp. 17–23)

- La fórmula ideal de contratación es diferente por contexto de comprador, pero el proceso de ingenierizar es idéntico: establecer teoría → definir evaluación → puntuar candidatos → iterar con datos
- Análisis de regresión sobre candidatos históricos revela predictores verdaderos de éxito (no intuición)
- Hallazgo: vendedores inteligentes y serviciales superan vendedores agresivos en era del comprador empoderado; Internet ha desplazado poder hacia buyer

**Cita ancla** (p. 22): «Statistics suggest salespeople who are intelligent and helpful, rather than aggressive and high-pressure, are most successful.»

### Cap. 2 · Five Traits Great Salespeople Have (pp. 24–34)

- En HubSpot: coachability, curiosidad, prior success, inteligencia, work ethic fueron predictores top de éxito
- Coachability = capacidad de absorber y aplicar coaching en vivo (probada con role-play + feedback inmediato en entrevista)
- Curiosidad = hacer preguntas abiertas para entender contexto del prospect, no "show up and throw up" pitch


### Cap. 3 · Finding Top-Performing Salespeople (pp. 35–43)

- Gran vendedores nunca necesitan aplicar a empleo; requiere estrategia de sourcing **pasivo**, no job boards
- Solución: construir agencia de recruiting dentro de empresa (recruiter con incentivos de agencia) > contratar agencia externa
- LinkedIn sourcing: busca por zip code, job title, school quality, company; screen por indicadores (rankings, longevidad, alignment); engage vía intro mutua o cold email; "forced referral" (revisar conexiones del nuevo hire)

**Cita ancla** (p. 35): «Great salespeople never need to apply for a job.»

### Cap. 4 · The Ideal First Sales Hire (pp. 44–49)

- SVP of Sales: NO (hesitancy, falta recent frontline, baja pace)
- #1 Salesperson: Posible pero necesita manager que coaching (falta leadership experience)
- **Entrepreneur (IDEAL)**: instinto entrepreneurial para product/market fit, sales fundamentals, leadership potential. Débil: sin sales management experience
- Clave: valor crítico no es revenue primaria sino **acelerar hacia product/market fit**

**Cita ancla** (p. 47): «The most critical value from your first sales hire comes not from the first»

### Cap. 5 · Setting Up a Predictable Sales Training Program (pp. 50–60)

- Problema: ride-alongs de un top performer (superpower única) confunden a nuevos hires. Betty = rapport builder, Bob = high activity; si Betty entrena a Bob, fracasa.
- Solución: Sales Methodology con 3 elementos (Buyer Journey, Sales Process, Qualifying Matrix) enseñados por especialistas
- Predictibilidad: exams (100 preguntas, product knowledge) + certifications (role-plays, rubrics 1-5-7-10, trainers externos no hiring managers)
- Iteración: feedback 6 meses post-hire, correlación training scores vs field performance, ajustes por evolución del negocio


### Cap. 6 · Manufacturing Helpful Salespeople Your Buyers Trust (pp. 61–66)

- Buyer empowered: investiga online, compara, prueba free. Sales debe ser trusted advisor, no script reader
- **Experiencial**: HubSpot new hires pasaban primeras semanas creando website + blog + social media → VIVÍAN como marketers → entendían dolor
- Modern selling = doctor/patient (diagnosis trust, prescription respect) no seller/buyer
- Personal brand via social media: Mark Roberge recibía 1 request/día por thought leadership (Twitter, LinkedIn, blogs, comentarios)

**Cita ancla** (p. 61): «The best-trained salespeople have experienced the day-to-day job of their potential customers.»

### Cap. 7 · Metrics-Driven Sales Coaching (pp. 68–78)

- Sales manager = sales coach; coaching = mayor leverage para productividad
- Error común: abrumar vendedor con múltiples skills feedback simultáneos
- Solución: diagnosis por métricas → 1 skill priorizado → custom coaching plan → verificar mes-a-mes si movió las agujas
- Cascada: 1er día mes (salesperson + manager diagnose + plan) → 2da mañana (managers + directors) → 2da tarde (directors + SVP)
- "Peeling back the onion": desglosar ratios altos (leads→demos) en sub-ratios (leads→connects, connects→demos) para aislar nodo exacto

**Cita ancla** (p. 68): «Effective sales coaching by sales managers is the most important lever to drive sales productivity.»

### Cap. 8 · Motivation through Sales Compensation Plans and Contests (pp. 79–90)

- Comp plan = **principal lever estratégico** para ejecutar strategy; debe evolucionar con stage negocio
- HubSpot 3 planes: 1) Hunting ($2/$1 MRR, clawback 4mo) → aceleró pero churn exploró, 2) Customer Success (quartiles $4/$3/$2/$1 por churn) → 70% drop churn, 3) Customer Commitment (payout 1/6/12 meses) → incentivó prepayment
- Criterios: Simple (clarity), Aligned (con goal año), Immediate (feedback rápido paycheck)
- Promotion tiers: criteria cuantificables (NO tenure); contests team-based, prizes team-based, daily standings, monthly timeframe

**Cita ancla** (p. 80): «The sales compensation plan drives the results of the business.»

### Cap. 9 · Developing Sales Leaders (pp. 91–100)

- Promover managers de dentro: NO contratar externo (buyer context único). 12-week sales leadership curriculum (7 tópicos: leadership style, feedback, coaching, conflict, change, team building, listening)
- Prerrequisitos: performance (6mo goal attainment), sales skills (well-rounded), leadership potential (contribute, mentor)
- Classroom → realidad: candidate hire + train + manage 1 salesperson (temporary double-duty, safer que prolonged "team lead")
- Potholes: weak time management (102 horas/semana), manager como "glorified salesperson" (take calls para closear), give up on hires too early (muchos "weak" se volvieron stars con 6mo coaching)

**Cita ancla** (p. 92): «Focus on leadership skills, rather than general sales management skills, when developing future managers internally.»

### Cap. 10 · Flip the Demand Generation Formula (pp. 102–113)

- Outbound (cold calls, mail, ads) vs Inbound (Google, social media). Buyers: nunca compran por outbound; compran por Google/social
- Google algoritmo: inbound links + social authority (no fake)
- 2 tácticas: 1) Quality content frequent (blogs, ebooks, webinars), 2) Participate en social media discussions donde buyers conversan
- Content production process: Hire journalist (NO domain experience required) + thought leadership committee (1 hour interview/week) → ebook + 3–4 blogs + dozens social posts
- Long-tail strategy: Niche topics (less competitive, qualified prospects) > generic head terms ("IT consultant", millions searches, wrong buyers)

**Cita ancla** (p. 103): «Today's buyer is empowered by the Internet. A modern demand generation strategy means less focus on interruptive outbound marketing and more focus on inbound marketing.»

### Cap. 11 · Converting Inbound Interest into Revenue (pp. 114–130)

- Marketing: NO pasar todos los leads; filtrar calidad (50 great + 450 weak → sales odia). Buyer Persona/Buyer Journey Matrix (y-axis: personas; x-axis: stages) mejor que lead scoring complicado
- Pass to Sales: Enterprise en Problem Education, Mid-Market en Solution Research, Small Business en Solution Selection (examples)
- Sales: 1) Scrap elevator pitch, lead with context (ebook mentioned, suggestions, case studies); final voicemail = "going negative" (highest callback), 2) Call low (intern = info coach), then call high (armed con contexto decision maker), 3) Prioritize por engagement (website visit, email open, competitor mention) NOT alphabetical, 4) Specialize team por inbound vs outbound

**Cita ancla** (p. 124): «The salesperson should treat the process like a dialogue.»

### Cap. 12 · Aligning Sales and Marketing (pp. 131–139)

- Sales-Marketing SLA = transforma relationship subjective en quantified, bidirectional accountability (quant mindset MIT)
- Marketing SLA: Define qualified lead → implied dollar value (conversion rate × avg purchase price), NOT raw leads count. Ejemplo: $12M implied value = 1000 demos + 3000 downloads (flexibility)
- Sales SLA: Call leads within 1 hour, optimal call attempts (small biz 5x, mid-market 8x, enterprise 12x por profitability analysis)
- Daily dashboards: Marketing vs ideal lead value trend, "Do Not Be on It" for sales (leads not touched, free trials <3x calls)
- Manage business DAILY, not monthly/quarterly

**Cita ancla** (p. 132): «Use the Sales and Marketing SLA to replace the subjective and qualitative aspects of the Sales/Marketing relationship with well-defined targets and quantified goals.»th well-defined quantified goals.»

### Cap. 13 · Technology to Sell Better, Faster (pp. 140–150)

- Sales tech históricamente built para leader, not salesperson → creates admin burden, adoption issues
- Modern sales tech debe: 1) Sell faster (eliminate admin, automate logging), 2) Sell better (capture buyer context, make available)
- Lead sourcing: Old = 10 clicks 10 min 1 lead. New (Sidekick) = Google sidebar con data + similar companies → 10 clicks 10 min 10 leads
- Prospecting: Prioritize por engagement (website visit, email open, competitor mention) NOT alphabetical; auto-log voicemails/emails/tasks; personalize context
- Engagement: Opportunity stages por buyer actions (confirmed summary, opened proposal, attended demo) NOT salesperson actions; tech recommends collateral por buyer persona/stage
- Automated reporting: Sales execs (forecast, funnel), marketers (lead working SLA, quality), salespeople (engaged prospects, activity vs team)

**Cita ancla** (p. 141): «Historically, sales technology has been built for the sales leader, not the salesperson. This technology does not work for salespeople.»

### Cap. 14 · Running Successful Sales Experiments (pp. 150–160)

- Filosofía: experimentation culture = continual improvement. Bottom-up innovation (frontline ideas, no top-down executives)
- Hackathons: frame problem → pitch ideas → select top 10 → breakout teams → exec board decision to fund
- Innovation culture: 1) Transparencia (financials, priorities, NPS shared), 2) 3 career tracks (leadership, functional expert, innovation), 3) Monthly innovation pipeline board review
- 5-step experiment formula: 1) Clear goal & measure, 2) Design execution (least time/investment), 3) Choose leader (passionate, skilled), 4) Assemble team (2+ top performers for "true negative"), 5) Check-ins monthly
- VAR program example: Pete Caputa nights/weekends → 5 customers target → 100-person team 6 años. GPCT experiment: top 5 salespeople + coach Andrew Quinn → 10% improvement → organic floor adoption

**Cita ancla** (p. 150): «Great teams have a core philosophy of continual improvement. A key ingredient to the sales acceleration formula is fostering a culture of experimentation.»

### Cap. 16 · Conclusion: Where Do We Go from Here? (pp. 162–164)

- 3 capas de learning: 1) Tácticas (hiring, training, management, inbound, technology), 2) Challenge norm en tu contexto único (innovar), 3) Cambiar percepción global sales (trusted advisor tipo doctor, no manipulador)
- Sales education oportunidad: No major en universidades, pero Fortune 500 CEOs vinieron de sales. Top students NOW interested (financial rewards, CEO paths)
- Stigma histórico: sales = manipulative, deceitful, unethical → need modern tactics para evolucionar
- **Call to action**: "Somebody holds most important element: improving global perception of sales itself. Perhaps it is you."
- Donación: All proceeds → BUILD-Boston (college prep, entrepreneurship, 95% seniors → college)

**Cita ancla** (p. 163): «Somebody out there holds the most important element of the sales acceleration formula: improving the underlying global perception of sales itself.»
