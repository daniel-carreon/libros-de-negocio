# Aaron Ross & Marylou Tyler — *Predictable Revenue* (2011)

## Tesis en tres líneas

1. La mayoría de empresas crecen asignando más vendedores a prospectar, lo que no funciona. El verdadero motor es crear un SISTEMA de generación de leads predictable, separado del cierre.
2. "Cold Calling 2.0" es prospecting sin llamadas frías: enviar emails personalizados cortos a ejecutivos genera 7–9% de respuesta, mucho mejor que cold calling tradicional, y es escalable como un proceso.
3. Predicción de ingresos = (Leads generados × Conversion Rate × Deal Size) + (Sales Cycle); requiere especialización de roles, métricas claras, paciencia y experimentación constante.

## Por capítulo

### Cap. 1 · Where the $100 Million Came From

- Aaron Ross creó un equipo de generación de leads en Salesforce.com que sumó $100M de ingresos recurrentes; no fue por contratar más vendedores, sino por construir un sistema replicable.
- El error fatal de CEOs: creer que "agregar salespeople = más crecimiento"; en realidad, el 80% de nuevos clientes viene de generación de leads, no de vendedores.
- Tres pilares: lead generation predictable, equipo de Sales Development dedicado 100% a prospecting, sistemas consistentes.
- La falta de presupuesto no es excusa: con una persona full-time + herramientas básicas se pueden crear $3M+ en pipeline nuevo.
- Cambiar de crecimiento orgánico (referrals iniciales) a crecimiento proactivo (campaigns) es difícil pero necesario cuando el boca-a-boca no escala.

**Cita ancla** (cap. 1): «Building a Sales Machine that creates ongoing, predictable revenue takes:\ 1.»

### Cap. 2 · Cold Calling 2.0 — Ramp Sales Fast Without Cold Calls

- Cold calling (llamar a desconocido sin previo contacto) es inefectivo y muere lentamente; el mercado cambió: buyers resisten más, tecnología permite tracking, accountability en marketing crece.
- Breakthrough: el cuello de botella real NO es convencer a decision maker, es ENCONTRARLO. Email masivo a 100 ejecutivos pidiendo referral interna genera 10% respuesta.
- En 2003 Aaron pasó de 2 oportunidades/mes (cold calling) a 11 en un mes con emails cortos + follow-up; el ROI a 1 año fue 3000% por persona (cada rep de $100k generaba $3M en contratos).
- Cold Calling 2.0 es sistemático, no heroico: repetible, medible, escalable, predecible (no actividades sino resultados).
- Tres principios: 1) No hacer cold calls (usar referrals internos vía email), 2) Medir resultados (opportunities/mes) no dials/día, 3) Todo es process-driven.

**Cita ancla** (cap. 2): «Mass emailing C-level Fortune 5000 executives, with specific kinds of emails, can generate 9%+ response rates»

### Cap. 3 · Executing Cold Calling 2.0

- Step 1: Ideal Customer Profile (ICP): describir qué empresas y qué roles son ideales, sus desafíos clave; esto enfoca el prospecting y reduce tiempo perdido.
- Step 2: Build list (OneSource, Jigsaw, ZoomInfo); Step 3: Send 50–100 emails/día dirigidas, esperar 5–10 respuestas/día (7–9% rate normal); Step 4: "Sell the Dream" (ayudar a veruna visión de solución).
- Step 5: Sales Development Rep genera, Account Executive cierra (CRÍTICO: roles separados; mezclarlos cae productividad 30%).
- SDR compensation: base + commission (50% por opportunities generadas/mes, 50% por deals cerrados); el mix incentiva ambos volumen y calidad.
- SFA (Salesforce.com best-in-class) es ESENCIAL: sin él no se puede auditar, escalar ni medir; permite dashboards, reporting, SFA adoption.

**Cita ancla** (cap. 3): «The commission is paid monthly. It is made up of two parts:\ - 50% depends on a goal for the number of qualified opportunities generated»

### Cap. 4 · Prospecting Best Practices

- Top 6 mistakes: 1) esperar resultados inmediatos (toma 2–4 sem cualificar opp), 2) escribir emails largos (móvil → cortos), 3) ir ancho no profundo (100 cias 1x vs 10 cias 10x), 4) rendirse con ideales, 5) no rendirse con malos, 6) medir dials/día no conversations/día.
- Best opening question: "Did I catch you at a bad time?" → muestra respeto, reduce defensiva, obtiene permiso.
- Account Status assembly line: Cold → Working → Active Opp → Check Back Quarterly → Dead Opp → Current Client → Bad Fit → Duplicate; permite organizar y focus.
- "Day in the Life": mañana = respond leads (urgent), tarde = calls + planning (important), noche = send campaigns (next morning fresh inbox).
- Mantén energía: break cada 90min, lunch con equipo, stop work a hora fija; overwork quema gente.

**Cita ancla** (cap. 4): «Did I catch you at a bad time?» (pregunta de apertura favorita)

### Cap. 5 · Sales Best Practices

- "Sell to Success" ≠ "Always Be Closing": el cierre artificial falló. Customers no creen que cierres, creen que resuelvas SU negocio. La visión compartida hace el cierre lógico no forzado.
- 9 formas de alargar ciclos: 1) targets equivocados, 2) sin proceso, 3) proceso diseñado pero no usado, 4) selling selfishly (vs solving), 5) selling bajo, 6) ignorar buying process del prospect, 7) no cuidar, 8) decir no mostrar, 9) no disqualify.
- Obsesiona sobre DECISION MAKING PROCESS no decision maker: ya no es dictatorial, es colaborativo; mapea quién influye, quién aprueba, cómo decide.
- Free trials: diseña CON prospect, comprende dolor primero, define éxito juntos, crea milestones, simplifica, asigna responsabilidades.
- "3 Hour 15 Min Sales Process": 15min call 1 (¿waste of time?) + 1hr discovery + 2hrs working session (vision conjunta) = decisión clara.
- Uncover TRUE problems (detrás de "desired solutions"): pregunta Why? 5 veces; "necesitamos CRM nuevo" es síntoma, "reports imprecisos al CFO" es raíz.
- Prospects should EARN proposals: no regales. Si piden pricing, propón scoping call con stakeholders; si declinan, no eran reales.

**Cita ancla** (cap. 5): «Giving out proposals too easily helps no one — though it might feel good to think, "Sent out another proposal today!"»

### Cap. 6 · Lead Generation and "Seeds, Nets & Spears"

- Seeds (alto cultivo, largo plazo, ↑↑ conversión): referrals, SEO, PR, content, word-of-mouth.
- Nets (marketing tradicional, ancho): email, eventos, PPC, social pagado.
- Spears (targeted, human effort): Cold Calling 2.0, top accounts, BD personali zada.
- Define términos: Prospects (names, no response) → Leads (registrados, respondieron) → Opportunities (qualified) → Clients (pagaron) → Champions (refieren).
- "Layers of Onion": deja que prospects elijan su camino; blog free → newsletter → webinar → trial → demo → propuesta. Reduce presión de control, mejora trust.
- Inbound métodos ranked: 1) Referrals, 2) Free Tools, 3) SEO, 4) Blogging, 5) Email Newsletters, 6) Webinars, 7) PPC, 8) Affiliate, 9) Social.
- Marketing Automation (Marketo case study): Lead Scoring (65+ points = hot), nurture automático 21 days, track behaviors (visits, clicks, opens), fast track < 5min response.

**Cita ancla** (cap. 6): «Seeds take a lot of time to cultivate and to ramp up, but once they get going, they are unbeatable»

### Cap. 7 · Seven Fatal Mistakes CEOs and Sales VPs Make

- **Fatal 1**: CEO no entiende sales/lead gen → objetivos arbitrarios, improviso. CEO DEBE educarse.
- **Fatal 2**: Account Executives prospectando → <20% tiempo, sólo strategic accounts. Prospecting = SDR 100%. AE = closing 100%.
- **Fatal 3**: Confiar que channels venden solos → No. Build direct sales first, channels vienen después.
- **Fatal 4**: Talent fumbles (hire, train, incentive mal) → ramp lento, churn alto. Solución: pattern hire, train interno, invest.
- **Fatal 5**: "Product-out" no "Customer-in" → diluve message. Tight = fuerte (manguera → rayo láser penetra).
- **Fatal 6**: Sloppy tracking → sin data no puedes mejorar. Track: 1) Leads created/mes, 2) Conversion rate L→Opp, 3) Opps created/mes (mejor métrica), 4) Conversion rate Opp→Closed, 5) Bookings (New/Add-on/Renewal).
- **Fatal 7**: Command-and-control → resistencia. Diseña sistemas que reps QUIERAN usar (return on THEIR time). Involve reps en diseño.
- **Bonus**: Neglect customer success → churn, referrals secos.

**Cita ancla** (cap. 7): «The bulk of prospecting into new accounts should be owned by a separate, dedicated prospecting role»

### Cap. 8 · Sales Machine Fundamentals

- 9 principles: 1) Be patient (4–12mo build machine), 2) Experiment constantly (A/B test), 3) No one-offs (si no es replicable, no hacerlo), 4) Get out of Excel (SFA = fuente verdad), 5) Sketch processes (flowchart), 6) Focus results not activity, 7) Track few important metrics (5 max), 8) Mind the "batons" (80% of problems en handoffs entre teams), 9) Baby steps (consistency > perfection).
- 4 core sales functions: 1) **Inbound Qualification** (Market Response Rep: website leads) → 2) **Outbound Prospecting** (Sales Development Rep: cold accounts) → 3) **Account Executives** (closers, new customers + current base) → 4) **Account Management/Success** (retention, renewal, happy customers).
- CRITICAL: **Specialize, specialize, specialize**. Lumping roles = loss of focus + talent development + visibility into problems.
- When to specialize: 2nd hire after 1st closer should be 1 SDR (even in small teams). Or use 80/20 rule: if reps spend >20% time on secondary function, break it out.
- Vision alternativa: "Pod teams" mini-business units (1 marketing + 2 inside sales + 2 field sales + 1 account mgr + 2 support + 1 presales) cada uno de mini-CEO, con own P&L.

**Cita ancla** (cap. 8): «This is so important that I mention it several times in this book: Specialize, Specialize, Specialize!»

### Cap. 9 · Cultivating Your Talent

- Farm team system: Market Response Rep (inbound) → Sales Development Rep (outbound) → Inside Account Executive → Field AE (mid-market) → Field AE (enterprise). Cada rol prepara para next.
- Best salespeople: listen >> talk, problem-solvers, understand customer industry/business, believe in product, integrity, get things done internally (navigate matrix).
- Hiring: talent + adaptability > experience. Raw talent ramped in 6–12mo, then surpasses veterans. Test 6mo starter role si duda.
- Commission-only: bad (95% companies) except financial services. No base → desperation → unethical behavior, churn. Invest en people → get commitment.
- Internal training > external: consistent, regular format + role-playing + feedback. Difference dramático en ramp time. CEO/VP must stay committed (else team doesn't care).
- Best training: role-play (stop, redo, practice) vs scripts (reps lose authenticity).
- SalesforceU model: self-managing weekly 1h–1.5h training (rotating leader each week). Agenda: 1) Sales Skills (public speak / role-play), 2) Quick Questions (Q&A rapid-fire), 3) Sales Skills 2, 4) New Best Practice, 5) Vertical Learning, 6) Feedback + Next Leader.

**Cita ancla** (cap. 9): «The best salespeople are the ones that have grown up in your company, and know it, your products, your customers, inside and out.\ \ \»

### Cap. 10 · Leadership and Management

- 6 manager responsibilities: 1) Choose people (hire carefully, test 6mo if unsure), 2) Set Expectations (results not activities), 3) Remove Obstacles (clear territories, comp plans, fairness), 4) Inspire (public/private praise, learning, career growth), 5) Work for Your People (not the reverse), 6) Improve It (reflect, iterate).
- V2MOM (Vision, Values, Methods, Obstacles, Metrics) = Salesforce.com planning ritual. Each level: corp, team, individual. Deep alignment top-down. Invest 80–100h exec level.
- Retaining stars: 12 key measures (know expectations, have materials, do best work, recognition, supervisor cares, encouragement, opinions count, mission important, coworkers committed, best friend, progress talks, learn & grow).
- Why reps resist: 1) People hate orders → design instead, 2) Coercion = shortcut, not solution, 3) Complexity grows → inflection point hairball. Solution: involve reps in design (they'll own it).
- Self-managing teams: ask "If manager disappeared tomorrow, how would team operate?" Then: Eliminate 80%, Automate, Outsource, then Distribute. Create Team Leads (rotating, 20% of comp) + Functional Leads (Goals Lead, Hiring Lead, Training Lead, etc.).
- Transparent compensation: publish all comp public (eliminates errors, builds trust, people see who does best and why).
- SFA adoption: 10 ways = CEO models, clean UI, comp depends on SFA data, communicate why, customize by role, train day 1, peer pressure ("if not in SFA, doesn't exist"), training online, 1:1 coaching, mobile app.

**Cita ancla** (cap. 10): «If it's not in the SFA system, it doesn't exist."»
