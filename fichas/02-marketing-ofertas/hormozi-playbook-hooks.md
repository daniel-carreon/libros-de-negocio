# Alex Hormozi — *$100M Playbook: Hooks* (2025)

> Ficha de lectura: mapa para ir al libro, no sustituto. Páginas = número de página **del PDF** (el que muestra el visor, no el impreso), salvo que `citar_por` diga capítulo.

## Tesis en tres líneas

1. **Hook = primeras palabras/segundos que capturan atención; diferencia entre bomba y éxito es solo el hook**: Larry King infomercial cambió solo opening sentence ("Have you ever looked in mirror...?") en vez de ego-driven intro, resultado = $M ventas vs. bomba anterior; 80 cents de cada dollar advertising es el hook.

2. **Hook tiene dos partes inseparables: call out (atención + relevancia) + value promise (si consumes obtendrás beneficio)**; cocktail party effect = cerebro filtra ruido masivo, hook mata señal; audiencia consume si cost < perceived benefit, hook estructura ambos.

3. **Proceso 70-20-10 aloca recursos: 70% proven hooks (más de qué funciona), 20% winner-adjacent (permutaciones prometedoras de otros nichos), 10% craziest ideas (risky pero huge wins si conectan)**; resultado = mix conservador + exploratorio que genera 121+ hooks probados y tipología (statements 47.9%, commands 21.5%, questions 9.9%, exclamations 9.1%, lists 5.8%, stories 3%, conditionals 2.5%).

## Por capítulo

### Cap. 1 · Intro - Larry King story (líneas 74–137)

- Dean Graziosi cuenta: intentó replicar estilo Larry King para Joel Olsteen infomercial, bomba; 10 años después consigue Larry King real, pero ad también bomba
- Análisis: todo idéntico excepto opening sentence; problema: hook ego-driven ("multi-millionaire, bestseller") vs. audience-driven ("Have you ever thought you should be further ahead?")
- Solución: una toma más, solo hook nuevo, resultado = monster success, $M ventas
- Lección: "I spent as much thinking about first sentence as everything else combined" = hook obsession is correct

**Cita ancla** (cap. 1): «You gotta nail the hook or nothing else matters.»

### Cap. 2 · What's A Hook? (líneas 143–151)

- Definición: whatever gets attention de audiencia; todo content/ads/outreach tiene hook
- Good hook = personas notan AND prestan atención ("Oh, this is for me")
- Goal: específico enough para right people, amplio enough para many de ellos

**Cita ancla** (cap. 2): «If nobody notices your ad, then nobody buys your stuff.»

### Cap. 3 · Importance (líneas 152–162)

- Hook = 80 cents de cada advertising dollar (David Ogilvy)
- Mastering hooks = 5x-100x negocio sin cambiar nada más
- Skill escala: si 10h advertising, 8h en hooks
- Vast majority ignore hooks, therefore massive opportunity

**Cita ancla** (cap. 3): «If you know how to write good hooks, you can *easily* 5x, 10x, and sometimes 100x the amount of people that pay attention to your»

### Cap. 4 · How Do Hooks Work? (líneas 164–189)

- Dos partes: 1) Call out = agarra atención, "This is for me", cocktail party effect (single sound pierces through noise, tu nombre en club ruidoso), 2) Hook itself = promesa de valor (si consumes = beneficio)
- Condición explícita: "If [TARGET] [CONDITION] [REWARD]"
- Condición implícita: visual + emotional communication
- Audience consume si cost de consumir < benefit percibido; hook sets up esas condiciones

**Cita ancla** (cap. 4): «Scientists call it the 'cocktail party effect.'»

### Cap. 5 · Types of Hooks - Verbal (líneas 191–227)

- Siete tipos verbal: labels ("Local business owners, I have gift"), questions (yes/open), conditionals (If scenario…), commands (Read this if…), statements (The smartest thing…), lists (28 ways to…), narratives (One day…), exclamations (Ahhhhh… blueprint)
- Nonverbal: sounds (dropped dishes vs. cling-cling champagne flute) + visuals
- Best = verbal + nonverbal together; platform-dependent

**Cita ancla** (cap. 5): «Think the first few seconds of a video, or the first thing you hear, or the first line or image your eyes see.»

### Cap. 6 · Hook Creation Process - 70-20-10 (líneas 234–350)

- Google's resource allocation: 70% core (proven), 20% emerging (promising), 10% big ideas (risky)
- 70% = usar hooks that already work (own winners, free content winners, others' ads, others' content); stabiliza income while exploring
- 20% = winner-adjacent (outliers tools, model concepts from other niches que parecen promising); paste en slide deck + brainstorm repackage
- 10% = totally new (risky, creative freedom, document wins/losses); si wins → va al 70% bucket

**Cita ancla** (cap. 6): «70% - Core Business. You put the largest chunk of effort into doing 'core activities.'»

### Cap. 7 · Testing Hooks (líneas 352–372)

- Traducción en recording según formato: ads (10 hooks per ad content), shorts (winning tweets), long (3-4 hooks pick best), emails (split test)
- Para startup sin past content: make bunch → top 10% = copy, bottom 10% = don't repeat
- "Let the data do the teaching" sin necesidad expert previo

**Cita ancla** (cap. 7): «Let the data do the teaching. Most people have no idea anyways.»

### Cap. 8 · 121 Best Performing Hooks (líneas 378–657)

- Analysis de 121 hooks desde ads, content, email, Twitter
- Distribution: Statements 47.9% (mayoría), Commands 21.5%, Questions 9.9%, Exclamations 9.1%, Lists 5.8%, Stories 3%, Conditionals 2.5%
- Colección categorizada por plataforma (Ads, YT, IG, Email, Twitter)
- Ejemplos reales = copiar estructura, adaptar a negocio propio

**Cita ancla** (cap. 8): «Real quick question… Can I have your email address?»

### Cap. 9 · Why Nail Hooks (líneas 663–684)

- Beneficios: más clicks → más revenue, mejores clicks → incluso más revenue, cold traffic se unlock, nuevos mercados profitable
- Costo de no hacerlo: wasted ad spend, peores customers, scale cap, no expansion

**Cita ancla** (cap. 9): «Nailing them has 2x-5x-10x'd many businesses overnight.»

### Cap. 10 · Hook Checklist (líneas 704–741)

- First time: look tops ads/content → isolate hooks → write 50 favorites → use all
- After (ongoing 70-20-10): top 10% performers = top 5 → 70% next batch using top 5 → 20% permutations (visuals + words) → 10% new
- Formula para variedad: conditions, labels, questions, commands, statements, lists, stories
- Ejecutable, tearoff reference

**Cita ancla** (cap. 10): «Look at top 10% performers (top 5)»
