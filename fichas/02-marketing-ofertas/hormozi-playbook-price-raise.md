# Alex Hormozi — *$100M Playbook: Price Raise* (2025)

> Ficha de lectura: mapa para ir al libro, no sustituto. Citas = fragmentos originales en inglés.

## Tesis en tres líneas

1. Pricing es la palanca de negocio más poderosa: duplicar precios puede triplicar ganancia si solo pierdes 20% conversión; la mayoría no lo hace por miedo, no por realidad.
2. Sistema RAISE (Remind, Address, Invest, Soften, Explain) elimina fricción de price increases: comunica valor ya dado, enframe increases en contexto de mejora continua, ofrece loyalty discount expirado.
3. Businesses que no revisan precios anuales erosionan lentamente; los que optimizan atraen mejores clientes, reinvierten en producto, escalan ads, mantienen talento.

## Por capítulo

### Prólogo · Anécdota: Trey y el gym a $49/mes

- Trey: North Carolina gym a $49/mes (intentaba noble + gratuidad)
- Problems: worst customers, losing $2000/mes, can't run ads, stuck
- Crisis: "Trey, in 12 weeks you won't have a gym"
- Solution: triplicar precios (triple overnight)
- Result: -$2000/mes → +$4000/mes, solo 1 semana implementación
- Insight: no hace falta crisis; pricing raise = fast profit en cualquier negocio

**Cita ancla** (Prólogo): «Prices were going up. Not just a little, *triple*.»

### Cap. 1 · Why Raising Prices Is A Good Idea: Ciclos virtuosos vs. viciosos

- Secret: "everyone else is BROKE" → no copies
- Easiest: charge más for same stuff
- Fear: angry messages, sales drop, unworthiness
- Root cause: "poor people thinking" = race to bottom
- Rich entienden: "more charge = more make"
- Smart owners: reinvest en ads, product, customer care

**Ciclo Vicious** (Price ↓):
- Emotional investment ↓, perceived value ↓, results ↓, demandingness ↑, service ↓

**Ciclo Virtuous** (Price ↑):
- Emotional investment ↑, perceived value ↑, results ↑, demandingness ↓, service ↑

**Cita ancla** (Cap. 1): «If you have to have a prayer session before raising the price by a tenth of a cent, > then you’ve got a terrible business”»

### Cap. 2 · My Rules For Raising Prices: 9 rules foundation

1. **Don't grandfather/lifetime deals**: Value depends price; if value up → price up. Never lock customers.

2. **Never one-time lifetime access**: Forever > what they paid once. Model success (no large company does this).

3. **Insecure early OK**: As improve + brand grow → price grow. Don't hate early adopters.

4. **Raise at least once yearly**: Warren Buffet owned Sees Candies; raised 10% avg/year, sometimes 17%.

5. **Test on new customers first**: Data on ROI + confidence for rest of base.

6. **Meet if raise >50%**: Talking with biggest raises helps retention.

7. **Fear not**: Shopify competitor $30 → $3000 (100x), lost 1 customer. "If people see value they stay."

8. **Pair with launch (within 90 days)**: Frame as early adopter pricing; draft off momentum.

9. **Do the math**: Know breakeven % of customers you can lose and still profit.

**Cita ancla** (Cap. 2): «If people see the value they will stay.»

### Cap. 3 · How To Pick Your Price: Pricing tables + analytics

- Best price = highest profit (not most sales)
- Formula: Price × Conversion Rate × LTV = Total Return
- Example table: $10 → $20 → $100
  - $10: 5% conv, $100 LTV = $500 total
  - $20: 4% conv (20% drop), $200 LTV = $800 total (60% more revenue)
  - $100: 2% conv (60% drop), 33% churn, $300 LTV = $600 total (20% more than $10, less than $20)

- Key insight: conversión y churn sube con precio, pero often not proportionally
- Si duplicas precio y pierdes 20% sales con todo igual → do it!
- Best: test entre $20 y $100 (try $39-$59)

**Cita ancla** (Cap. 3): «You may lose some but often not as much as you gain»

### Cap. 4 · The Perfect Price Raise Letter: RAISE framework

**R - Remind** (current value delivered):
- Personalize: calls attended, deliverables used, revenue, profit increase
- Format: "Over past 12 months we've added so much value to..."

**A - Address** (price change directly):
- Single sentence, rip off bandaid
- "For us to continue investing we need to increase prices"

**I - Invest** (frame in value context):
- Show what you'll do with extra $
- Categories: hiring, training, equipment, tech, facilities, service
- Pair each investment with "more good" (aligned, faster, easier, risk-free) or "less bad"
- NO lying; NO new expenses negating raise profit

**S - Soften** (loyalty discount expires 3-6 months):
- People prefer vanishing discount vs. raised price
- Raise NOW + discount that expires later = feels like gifting
- Show credit on invoices
- "We raising prices on new customers, but you special so credit today + raise future"

**E - Explain** (address concerns with PS):
- PS: "If makes you homeless/breaks business let me know figure something out"
- 3 types: See value (comply), Actually affected (extend), Were canceling anyway (pulled forward churn)

**Cita ancla** (Cap. 4): «if this will materially affect your ability to buy groceries let me know»

### Cap. 5 · Implementation: Template + Options + Checklist

**Two implementation approaches:**
1. Go all way up (like Trey)
2. Stair-step: discount falls off in 3 increments (ex $200/mo 4mo, $100/mo, $50/mo)

**For new customers:**
- Update price immediately; no grandfathering
- If complain "heard cheaper": "Good news - we keep making value better, 96% agreed, cheapest ever"

**For existing customers:**
- If price raise >50%, consider 1-on-1 calls
- Use video + community post if have group (turn off comments)

**Price Raise Checklist** (12 steps):
1. Decide increase
2. Test new customers
3. Segment old customers
4. Write 1-5 bullets current value
5. Tell price raise now
6. Write 3 bullets investments
7. Explain benefits
8. Give expiring discount
9. Offer personal response
10. Sign in ink if possible
11. Strong PS statement
12. Do individually if 50%+; stair-step available

**Cita ancla** (Cap. 5): «Cover the main points. Be bold. You'll survive»

### Cap. 6 · Why You Should Actually Do This: Virtuous business cycle

**When optimize pricing right:**
- Attract better customers
- More profit per customer
- Reinvest in experience
- Advertise more
- Unlock expensive acquisition channels
- Feel good about product
- Keep best talent

**When do wrong:**
- Wrong customers
- Erode profits
- Stop reinvesting
- Less money monthly
- Feel worse
- Lose talent

**Action:** Revisit pricing at least annually; make up inflation + other costs; get addicted to raises; keep improving product

**Cita ancla** (Cap. 6): «These playbooks are meant to be used, not studied. Make money. Have fun»

### Cap. 7 · My Gym Price Increase Letter: Real example (Trey)

- Longer, traditional tone vs. RAISE template
- Sections: gratitude + improvements coming

**Current efforts:** Hire coaches, education, equipment

**Coming investments:** Facility upgrades, member events, specialty programs, appointment-based sessions (3x/week)

**Price change:** Weekly billing; $10-$15/week increase; $39/week final

**Retention:** Refer-a-Friend program (1 free month per 12 referrals)

**Tone:** Personalized, community-focused, in-person for concerns, P.S. "I love you all"

- Note: Shorter RAISE template better, but this one worked for "hundreds of gyms"

**Cita ancla** (Cap. 7): «I hope I have your support in allowing us to continue to serve you in the best way we know how.»
