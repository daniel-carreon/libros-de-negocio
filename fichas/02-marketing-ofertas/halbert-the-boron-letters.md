# Gary C. Halbert — *The Boron Letters* (1984)

> Ficha de lectura: mapa para ir al libro, no sustituto. Páginas = número de página del PDF.

## Tesis en tres líneas

1. El marketing directo exitoso comienza observando qué compra la gente (no lo que dice), eligiendo mercados "hambrientos" (listas de mailing responsivas), y vendiendo lo que ya demandan—no lo que el vendedor cree que necesitan.

2. La estructu­ra de una pieza de direct mail debe parecer personal y editorial (no comercial), llevar al lector por pasos claros (AIDA: atención, interés, deseo, acción) y ocultar la intención de vender hasta que haya leído suficiente para desear el producto.

3. La mentalidad del vendedor—disciplina, conciencia de sí mismo (HALT), capacidad de observar comportamiento real, disposición a trabajar intensamente—determina el éxito más que cualquier técnica de copy o diseño.

- Comienza con salud porque "how you feel affects how you think"

### Cap. 3 · Wednesday, June 13, 1984 (pp. 15–21)

- Fasting un día por semana como herramienta de auto-disciplina; genera patrón de "ordenar" la semana
- Dieta recomendada: fruta, verduras crudas, cereales de salvado, leche descremada, proteína magra
- Cuento del gato Crackers tormenting un antelope ground squirrel como metáfora de poder
- "There is no justice. There is only power"—lección crítica sobre realismo

**Cita ancla** (p. 16): «There is no justice. There is only power.»

### Cap. 4 · Thursday, June 14, 1984 (pp. 22–26)

- Resumen de hábitos: road work, fasting, dieta balanceada
- Auto-reliance: nunca depender de otros para ejecutar (ej: cortar propias verduras, no confiar en familia)
- Desarrollo de brazos con presses y curls; cuerpo lean and hard, no bulging
- "Try things at least twice"—segundo intento es mucho más fácil, la mayoría rinde a primer fracaso

**Cita ancla** (p. 23): «I think a man looks best (and I think most women would agree with me) when he is lean an hard - not bulging and»

### Cap. 5 · Friday, June 15, 1984 (pp. 29–33)

- Defensive behavior invites aggressive action: aspecto débil atrae depredadores
- Fortaleza (brazos, postura, señales) disuade sin necesidad de agresión
- Self-reliance es más satisfactorio que depender de compasión ajena
- Importancia de eliminar gente negativa del círculo (weeds in a garden)

**Cita ancla** (p. 28): «It is far better to rely on your own strength instead of somebody else's compassion»

### Cap. 6 · Saturday, June 16, 1984 (pp. 35–38)

- Dinero es byproduct de entusiasmo, no objetivo directo
- Contratar por actitud, no calificaciones; el entusiasta aprende rápido
- **Estudiante de mercados (no de técnicas)**; observar qué compran, no lo que dicen
- Ejemplo cerveza: 80% dijeron premium, pero compraron regular
- National Enquirer: largest selling newspaper mundo pero "nadie" admite leerlo; gente miente

**Cita ancla** (p. 35): «MORE PEOPLE READ THE "NATIONAL ENQUIRER" IN ONE SINGLE WEEK THAN HAVE EVER READ THE BIBLE IN THE LAST 2,000 YEARS!»

### Cap. 7 · Sunday, June 17, 1984 (pp. 40–45)

- 10 calidades de mailing lists de peor a mejor: phonebook → professionals → mail order buyers → repeat buyers → big spenders → recent big spenders → recientes+working → mejor broker → **your own satisfied customer list** (mejor de todos)
- Lesson: "Sell people what they want to buy!" así de simple, así ignorado
- Observe ads que repiten = hit a nerve; réplica el nerve pero hazlo mejor

**Cita ancla** (p. 40): «Possibility #5 - We could mail our promotion to a group of wealthy people who have ordered some other investment book»

### Cap. 8 · Monday, June 18, 1984 (pp. 46–49)

- Recency, Frequency, Unit of Sale (RFU) criterio para evaluar listas
- Recency es el más crítico (hotline buyers, 30-90 day recent)
- Hot mail order publications revelan dónde publicar
- "All You Have To Do Is Observe!"—observe repeated ads, list sizes, mailing patterns

**Cita ancla** (p. 47): «All You Have To Do Is Observe!»

### Cap. 9 · Tuesday, June 19, 1984 (pp. 52–56)

- Cuándo trabajar vs descansar: si "crummy" continúa; si sick paramos
- "Most of the world's work is done by people who didn't feel much like getting out of bed"
- Gente no admite comprar por mail (como Enquirer), pero lo hacen; verdad está en wallets, no mouths
- Ejemplo: pregnant women list 120K/mes, $19.95 report, 5% response = $58,200/mes profit

**Cita ancla** (p. 51): «Therefore, truth, my good son, can be determined NOT by how people use their mouths, but rather, how they use their wallets»

### Cap. 10 · Wednesday, June 20, 1984 (pp. 58–63)

- Customización multiplica efectividad: "How Architects Can Make Money" vs genérico
- Ejemplo 33K chiropractors con offer customizado = $9,920 profit en <30 días
- Concepto "winning formula": reutilizable en múltiples vertientes (plumbers, doctors, etc.)
- Double customization: "How cardiologists can buy L.A. real estate without down payment"

**Cita ancla** (p. 60): «How To Buy L.A. Real Estate With No Money Down»

### Cap. 11 · Thursday, June 21, 1984 (pp. 64–70)

- 5 Steps crear DM promotion: lista → producto → research → breakthrough idea → formula
- AIDA formula: Attention, Interest, Desire, Action
- Assemble file con list cards, DM pieces, space ads, libros, headlines
- Cuando atascado: keep moving; "You don't have to get it right, you just have to get it moving"

**Cita ancla** (p. 65): «Did you know that there is now a way for a cardiologist to buy L.A. real estate»

### Cap. 12 · Friday, June 22, 1984 (pp. 71–76)

- **A-PILE vs B-PILE concept**: gente divide correo en personal (abre 100%) vs comercial (abre ~%50)
- Sobre: debe parecer personal (stamp live 20¢, no label, no bulk rate)
- Baggie con tierra attached = attention grabber relevante que funciona
- Lead reader by hand with small instructions: "(over)", "(go to page 7)"

**Cita ancla** (p. 71): «make the envelope look personal. (Or at least we will take pains so it doesn't look commercial.) Here is what our envelope should look like»

### Cap. 13 · Saturday, June 23, 1984 (pp. 79–81)

- Day/time specifics en letter (Friday 6:30 p.m. June 12) = bonding, immediacy, importance (like telegram)
- Personal salutation "Dear Bond" > "Dear Sir" o "Dear Occupant"
- Reply envelopes: SRE (stamped $200/M cost) vs BRE (cheaper, pero comercial) vs PSH (cheaper + personal)

**Cita ancla** (p. 78): «Dear Bond" gets a certain quality of focused attention that is not enjoyed by "Dear Sir"»

### Cap. 14 · Sunday, June 24, 1984 (pp. 83–88)

- SRE para inducir guilt: "if you can't, would you please tell me...I put stamp"
- Si persona comienza escribir "no", sometimes cambia idea y ordena
- BRE disadvantages: slow mail, telegraph commercial, no guilt induction
- PSH (Place Stamp Here) = sweet spot entre economía y personalidad

**Cita ancla** (p. 84): «The reason they are cheaper is that you only pay the postage and BRE fee for those envelopes that are actually mailed back to you»

### Cap. 15 · Monday, June 25, 1984 (pp. 89–96)

- Attention grabber debe ser RELEVANT; no bait-and-switch (fake traffic ticket = anger, no sale)
- Baggie con arena para real estate = tie-in natural, "could be passport to financial independence"
- "Picture with pleasure": reader visualiza playa (feel sand, smell salt air, feel sun)
- Hide order card/brochure hasta después letter: fold trick con nota azul "Open for special info"

**Cita ancla** (p. 89): «could very well be your passport to complete financial independence»

### Cap. 16 · Tuesday, June 26, 1984 (pp. 98–101)

- Happy Birthday Bond (16 años)
- Assemble file: list cards, report, DM pieces, space ads, libros, headlines
- **Nugget notes**: pequeños fragmentos, a veces palabra sola, frases sin sentido OK
- Star rating: *, **, *** según importancia; dejar reposar 1-2 días
- AIDA formula: Attention, Interest, Desire, Action (estructura secuencial probada)

**Cita ancla** (p. 99): «STOP 10:39 START AGAIN 10:51 By formula, I mean you should work within a proven sequential outline like AIDA»

### Cap. 17 · Wednesday, June 27, 1984 (pp. 104–108)

- AIDA deep dive: attention (crucial, grabbers), interest (facts), desire (benefits enumerated), action (be clear, specific)
- Benefits money: new car, nice house, peace of mind, vacations, attract opposite sex, leisure
- Devote 25% o más del ad al closing; tell exactly what to do, cuando, cómo
- Assignment: copy facelift ad en handwriting

**Cita ancla** (p. 104): «Tell him what he will get if he hurries and tell him what he will lose if he»

### Cap. 18 · Thursday, June 28, 1984 (pp. 110–116)

- Copy winning ads en handwriting = imprint proceso en mind/body (ejemplo: Arthur Haley + freighter travel)
- Simple words ("get" not "procure"), short sentences/paragraphs, transition words, Q&A
- "The Best Way To Become A Good Writer Is By Writing Good Writing!"
- **"Write for money, not applause"**; use word pictures (vicariously experience benefits)

**Cita ancla** (p. 111): «Listen up dummy. “If you are writing for applause... you will go home with empty pockets»

### Cap. 19 · Friday, June 29, 1984 (pp. 117–122)

- Eye relief & layout = can get 500% más readership; layout shouldn't "notice"
- Ads should look "editorial" (like rave review), not obviously commercial
- Coat of Arms letter: 1-page, 361 words, 7,156,000 customers
- Failed: 4-color 70-item catalog; failed: 3-item brochure; succeeded: personal letter con 1 photo snapshot = 40 MILLION DOLLARS

**Cita ancla** (p. 119): «That letter brought in 40 mil while my other more professional attempts fell flat on their rears»

### Cap. 20 · Saturday, June 30, 1984 (pp. 123–125)

- "You Never Get A Second Chance To Make A First Impression"
- Decision hecha en fracción de segundo (pupil dilation involuntario)
- Reader hooks or loses INSTANTLY when first looks, no cuando lee
- Crisp look, clean layout, contrast, easy-to-read; upbeat pictures; quality paper (white), large stamps, serif typeface, RED second color

**Cita ancla** (p. 121): «You either hook a reader or lose him when he very first looks at your ad»

### Cap. 21 · Sunday, July 1, 1984 (pp. 127–130)

- Bad ego decisions > grave mistakes en vida; confrontación con roommate sobre radio
- **HALT** = Hungry, Angry, Lonely, Tired; NUNCA hacer decision en estado HALT
- Qué hacer cuando emotivo: write, run, walk, jog, talk (pero careful con a quién)
- "You don't have to get it right, you just have to get it MOVING"; process > content

**Cita ancla** (p. 128): «You Don't Have To Get It Right You Just Have To Get It MOVING!»

### Cap. 22 · Monday, July 2, 1984 (pp. 132–137)

- Propositions = the deal; necesita "reason why" para believability
- Common reasons: going out of business, fire sale, losing lease
- BEST reason: "I am offering because you (by virtue of unique circumstance) are so special"
- Ejemplo: family crest plaque letter con explanation de por qué special = 40 Million In Sales
- Power word: "bushel of money" > "dramatically increase sales volume"

**Cita ancla** (p. 132): «I Am Offering You This Deal Because You By Virtue Of Some Unique Circumstance Are So Special»

### Cap. 23 · Tuesday, July 3, 1984 (pp. 138–142)

- Read copy aloud = encontrar bumps, verbally stumble = rewrite smooth
- "Advertising writing needs to be best writing. Flow without bump or bubble"
- Business model con bankers: identify list → product idea → discuss → $5K test 2-5M → si good $50K 100K → rollout
- 7 Steps DM Success (iterative, market-first)
- Most common mistake: product first, market second (BACKASSWARDS!)

**Cita ancla** (p. 140): «You Must Always Find A Market First And Then Concentrate On A Product!»

### Cap. 24 · Wednesday, July 4, 1984 (pp. 143–147)

- Guilt a Bond por no hacer roadwork; énfasis en "KEEP ON KEEPING ON" incluso en adversidad
- Awareness = diferencia entre success y failure
- Gente camina "heads in sand, lost in fog"; en prison = gobbled up weak fish
- Stay updated: trade journals, competition, hot publications, swipe file, classic books, idea files, network
- "I Work Like Crazy!"—la razón detrás del éxito percibido como "genius"
- Poker analogy: same hands dealt, difference is how played (alert? exploit good? dump bad?)
- Believability via exact details (77.6% vs "most", 37.5 lbs vs "lots")

**Cita ancla** (p. 143): «if I really am so good, there is at least one reason for it that everybody misses.»

### Cap. 25 · Thursday, July 5, 1984 (pp. 149–152)

- Stopping letters para review, outline, type, Rule 35 motion, work con bankers 50/50
- **IMPACT** = impressión en target; average American: 1,500 commercial messages/día pero few tienen impact
- Best technique: attach relevant items (dollar bill = 90%+ response tens of millions; Japanese penny = working crazy)
- WARNING: no cheap tricks, fit naturally; ejemplo malo: fake traffic ticket = remember con anger = NO SALE
- Ejemplos buenos: eagle feather (extinction), Mexican peso (inflation), computer chip (electronics)

**Cita ancla** (p. 150): «But He Will Remember The Company With Distaste! He Will Remember That This Is A Company That Scared Him And Made Him Angry»

### Cap. 26 · Friday, July 5, 1984 (pp. 159–165) — ÚLTIMA (primeras 25)

- Ice cup confrontation con "The Rodent"; cuando "off" = vulnerable
- Signs de estar "off": time sense incorrect, clumsiness, HALT state
- Being weak/vulnerable COMMUNICATED; en prison = especially dangerous
- Strategy: lay low, fast, pump iron, send stronger vibes
- People smell weakness/success; "You can't fake it. Not for long. You've got to be it!"
- Final: precious good times/people, take care special relationships
- Resolve: go underground, strengthen quietly, become expert diffusing explosive situations

**Cita ancla** (p. 160): «And you can't fake it. Not for long. You've got to be it!»

## Para Daniel

Estos 25 primeros capítulos son un blueprint completo para el diagnóstico RACE: identificar el "starving crowd" (mercado hambriento) es paso 1, luego diagnosticar su dolor exacto (A-pile vs B-pile, AIDA), diseñar la oferta con "reason why" creíble, y cerrarlo con claridad extrema. Toda la metodología de Gary—desde HALT (saber si estás mental OK para decidir) hasta SRDS (investigación de mercado real)—aplica directo a la primera llamada y la recomendación en 24h; usa "Sell People What They Want To Buy" y la customización (una propuesta por tipo de cliente) para esa página de 3 acciones.
