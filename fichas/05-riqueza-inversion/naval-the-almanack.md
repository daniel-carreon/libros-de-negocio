# Naval Ravikant — *The Almanack of Naval Ravikant* (2020)

> Ficha de lectura: mapa para ir al libro, no sustituto. Compendio de transcripciones editadas de podcasts, entrevistas y tweets de Naval Ravikant entre 2008-2019, compiladas por Eric Jorgenson. Páginas = número de página del PDF (el que muestra el visor).

## Tesis en tres líneas

1. **Riqueza es un conjunto de habilidades** (conocimiento específico + accountability + leverage) que se desarrollan mediante juegos de largo plazo con gente íntegra; no es suerte ni producto de trabajo duro ciego, sino de judgment y dirección clara.
2. **Felicidad es un estado mental aprendido** (no heredado) que requiere remover el deseo de cosas externas, aceptar la realidad tal como es, vivir en el presente, y construir hábitos deliberados; se logra por paz interna, no por circunstancias externas.
3. **Las decisiones fundamentales son pocas** (dónde vives, con quién estás, qué haces) pero dominan la trayectoria; la vida moderna permite escapar de jerarquías mediante equidad, código y media permissionless, y el verdadero éxito viene de ser auténtico a ti mismo, no de imitar a otros.

## Por capítulo

### Part I: Wealth (pp. 23–103)

#### Understand How Wealth Is Created (pp. 25–36)

- Riqueza ≠ dinero (transferencia de tiempo) ≠ estatus (lugar en jerarquía social)
- Riqueza = activos que generan ingresos mientras duermes; es resultado de habilidades, no suerte
- Productizarte: combina autenticidad (tu unicidad) + leverage (escala) + accountability (riesgo bajo tu nombre)
- Sociedad recompensa lo que quiere pero no sabe conseguir; si es obvio, ya está resuelto
- Las tres grandes decisiones: dónde vives, con quién trabajas, qué haces; todas las demás son detalle

**Cita ancla** (p. 26): «Seek wealth, not money or status. Wealth is having assets that earn while you sleep.»

#### Find and Build Specific Knowledge (pp. 33–40)

- Conocimiento específico = lo que hacías sin esfuerzo de niño; no se enseña en escuela, se aprende por curiosidad y pasión
- No se puede entrenar a otros en esto (si se pudiera, ya te reemplazaron); es combinatoria única de DNA + crianza + respuesta propia
- El internet amplió masivamente el espacio de carreras; ahora puedes encontrar tu audiencia y construir riqueza en nichos
- Escape competencia siendo auténtico: nadie puede competirte en ser tú (Joe Rogan, Calvin and Hobbes)
- Aprendizaje perpetuo es la habilidad más importante; mejor dominar fundaciones (aritmética, persuasión, presencia) que especializaciones profundas

**Cita ancla** (p. 36): «Every human is different. Don't copy.»

#### Play Long-Term Games with Long-Term People (pp. 40–42)

- Compound interest aplica a negocios, reputación y relaciones, no solo dinero
- Trabajar con gente íntegra durante años crea confianza que transforma negociaciones simples y deals automáticos
- 99% del esfuerzo es "desperdiciado" en el sentido que no compone; encuentra el 1% que sí compone y ve all-in
- Reputación es capital: Warren Buffett genera deals infinitos solo porque gente confía en su integridad

**Cita ancla** (p. 26): «You must own equity—a piece of a business.»

#### Take on Accountability (pp. 43–44)

- Accountability bajo tu propio nombre = riesgo + credibilidad + leverage
- Sociedades modernas no tienen debtors' prison; puedes fallar públicamente sin ruina total
- Naval arriesgó carrera con filosofía y crypto; fue juzgado pero ganó reputación como pensador original
- Sin accountability no hay incentivos; sin incentivos no hay credibilidad

**Cita ancla** (p. 28): «Embrace accountability, and take business risks under your own name.»

#### Build or Buy Equity in a Business (pp. 45–47)

- Sin ownership de una pieza del negocio, no hay path a libertad financiera
- Salaried job (incluso abogados, doctores) = inputs ligados a outputs; cuando duermes no ganas; cuando jubilas no ganas
- Ownership del upside requiere tomar downside; eso es lo que pagadores evitan darte
- Stock options, fundar, invertir = rutas a riqueza; wage-earning no

**Cita ancla** (p. 45): «If you don't own a piece of a business, you don't have a path towards financial freedom.»

#### Find a Position of Leverage (pp. 48–64)

- Tres tipos de leverage: labor (peor, requiere gestión), capital (moderno, requiere que alguien te lo dé), code/media (nuevo, permissionless)
- Código permite army de robots trabajando gratis; new billionaires (Bezos, Zuckerberg, Gates, Jobs) tienen code leverage
- Real estate ejemplo: laborer ($15/h) → contractor → developer → fund manager → tech+RE company (billones); cada nivel = más leverage + accountability + knowledge específico
- Warren Buffett: toma un año decidiendo, un día actuando; esa decisión dura décadas
- SELL o BUILD: si sabes ambas, eres imparable

**Cita ancla** (p. 29): «Code and media are permissionless leverage. They're the leverage behind the newly rich.»

#### Get Paid for Your Judgment (pp. 58–64)

- El mundo paga por judgment, no por horas trabajadas
- 10% mejor judgment sobre $100B = $100M valor; leverage amplifica diferencias pequeñas en decision-making
- Warren Buffett cobra órdenes de magnitud más porque su judgment vale infinito leverage; reputation = infinite leverage
- Demostrated judgment + accountability + track record = credibilidad que atrae capital infinito

**Cita ancla** (p. 59): «Judgment especially demonstrated judgment with high accountability and a clear track record is critical.»

#### Prioritize and Focus (pp. 58–64)

- Set aspirational hourly rate (Naval: $5k/h incluso antes de tener dinero) y outsource todo < eso rate
- Riqueza vs dinero vs estatus son tres juegos diferentes; status es zero-sum (monkey tribes), riqueza es positive-sum
- Optimists hacen mejor long-term porque pueden hacer business con ilusiones de mutual upside
- Anti-wealth mindset previene riqueza porque tu espíritu no atrae dealmakers íntegros

**Cita ancla** (p. 63): «[78] Wealth creation is an evolutionarily recent positive-sum game. Status is an old zero-sum game. Those attacking wealth creation are often just seeking status»

#### Find Work That Feels Like Play (pp. 66–77)

- Retiro = stop sacrificing today for imaginary tomorrow (cuando hoy es completo en sí mismo); no es edad
- 3 formas de retirarse: pasivos > burn; burn = 0 (monk); doing algo que amas (no es about dinero)
- Art = creativity for its own sake; Navy trabaja 16h/day jugando porque otros trabajan hard = él gana
- Money = bottomless pit si lo lustas; lust for money bad para ti (paranoia, nunca enough)
- Sequence correcta: get rich → get fit/healthy → get happy (Buddha era príncipe, pudo dejar todo; ahora money permite libertad interna)

**Cita ancla** (p. 67): «Retirement is when you stop sacrificing today for an imaginary tomorrow. When today is complete, in and of itself, you're retired.»

#### How to Get Lucky (pp. 71–75)

- 4 tipos de suerte: blind (out of control) → hustle (generating motion/energy) → spotting (skilled, reconoces breaks) → character creates (build unique character, opportunity busca a ti)
- Deep-sea diver ejemplo: si eres el mejor en algo único, cuando surge oportunidad te buscan, pagan bien
- Warren Buffett: deals buscan a él porque integrity = infinite leverage
- Character + reputation = non-random luck

**Cita ancla** (p. 72): «Your character and your reputation are things you can build.»

#### Be Patient (pp. 74–76)

- Great people 20 años atrás: todos extremadamente successful; tuviste que darles tiempo
- No cuenta los días; si contas, te cansas antes que llegue el éxito
- Paciencia + specific knowledge + accountability + leverage = riqueza inevitable

**Cita ancla** (p. 31): «Apply specific knowledge with leverage and eventually, you will get what you deserve.»

### Building Judgment (pp. 78–107)

#### Judgment (pp. 79–82)

- Wisdom = knowing long-term consequences; judgment = wisdom applied to external problems
- Age of leverage: one correct decision wins everything
- Direction matters más que velocidad; pick right direction, start walking
- Hard work underrated, judgment underrated (Warren: año pensando, día actuando)

**Cita ancla** (p. 79): «In an age of leverage, one correct decision can win everything.»

#### How to Think Clearly (pp. 82–87)

- Clear thinker > smart; explicar a niño = saber de verdad (Feynman: explains math en 3 pages, counting to precalculus)
- Real knowledge = intrinsic, built ground-up; si usas fancy words, no sabes
- Deal with reality = remove ego, see things as they are
- Suffering = moment cuando no puedes negar reality; gente en dolor desea realidad diferente, no ve truth
- Need empty space/boredom para pensar; busy mind = no ideas

**Cita ancla** (p. 80): «Clear thinker is a better compliment than smart.»

#### Shed Your Identity to See Reality (pp. 85–87)

- Ego construido en primeros 20 años; resto de vida intentas hacerlo feliz
- Habits bundles en identity (soy X); uncondition y preguntar: ¿still serves me?
- Packed beliefs (Democrat, Catholic, American) suspicious; si todas tus beliefs aligned, very suspicious
- Contrarian belief propia = likely real; más ego/tribe la rechaza, más probablemente es truth

**Cita ancla** (p. 85): «Tension is who you think you should be. Relaxation is who you are.»

#### Learn the Skills of Decision-Making (pp. 87–91)

- Decision-making = everything; 80% vs 70% right = hundreds times más valued en leverage world
- Almost all biases = time-saving heuristics; para decisions importantes, discard memory + identity
- Radical honesty = congruencia entre think/say; si te mientes, crees tu propia mentira, disconnects from reality
- Warren Buffett rule: praise specifically, criticize generally (egos no trabajan against ti)

**Cita ancla** (p. 88): «Almost all biases are time-saving heuristics. For important decisions, discard memory and identity, and focus on the problem.»

#### Collect Mental Models (pp. 89–91)

- Brain = memory prediction machine; use principles no specific circumstances
- Evolution, game theory, Charlie Munger, Nassim Taleb, Benjamin Franklin = mental models sources
- Twitter/maxims = pointers a principles; compress learning en aphorisms
- Decision-making is leverage: small improvement judgment = massive value

**Cita ancla** (p. 90): «Mental models are really just compact ways for you to recall your own knowledge.»

#### Learn to Love to Read (pp. 95–102)

- Reading 1-2 hours/day = top .00001%; que solo accounts for material success Naval tiene + intelligence
- Read what you love until love reading; rereading > new books; pick one point from nonfiction
- "If they wrote make money, don't read it"; read originals (Darwin) not interpretations (Dawkins)
- Older problems = older solutions; health/peace/family → read ancient wisdom
- Foundations (math, science, philosophy) > junk; foundation critical because future ideas judged by it

**Cita ancla** (p. 98): «The number of books completed is a vanity metric.»

### Part II: Happiness (pp. 105–167)

#### Learning Happiness (pp. 107–111)

- Happiness = learned skill, not inherited; Naval: 10 años atrás 2–3/10, ahora 9/10
- Happiness = default state cuando nothing missing; remove sense of something missing = internal silence
- Positive thought holds negative thought within (duality); happiness ≠ positive thoughts
- Happiness = absence of desire, especially external; mind en movimiento = unhappy
- Nature neutral, mathematical laws; reality has no judgments; TÚ decides interpretation
- View self como bacteria = no expectation = no cause happy/unhappy

**Cita ancla** (p. 107): «Happiness is there when you remove the sense of something missing.»

#### Happiness is a Choice (p. 111)

- Mind malleable like body; memory + identity = burdens preventing freedom in present
- "If wisdom imparted words alone, done"; must verify yourself through experience

**Cita ancla** (p. 111): «Happiness, love, and passion aren't things you find—they're choices.»

#### Happiness Requires Presence (p. 112)

- Small % brain present; resto planning future o regretting past
- "Enlightenment = space entre thoughts"; puedes lograr momento a momento
- Anticipation for vices pulls to future; eliminar vices = easier estar present

**Cita ancla** (p. 112): «A very small percentage of your brain is focused on the present.»

#### Happiness Requires Peace (pp. 113–114)

- Happiness ≠ purpose; para Naval happiness = peace, not joy
- "Nexting" anxiety = always next thing; peace > joy
- Happy person = effortlessly interprets eventos without losing innate peace

**Cita ancla** (p. 113): «A happy person isn't someone who's happy all the time.»

#### Every Desire is a Chosen Unhappiness (pp. 114–117)

- Desire = contract to be unhappy until get what want; Buddha wisdom
- Naval new car: addicted to desiring, delusional que car brings happiness
- One big desire at a time; more desires = slowly destroying happiness
- Young: time + health - money; middle: money + health - time; old: money + time - health = trifecta impossible

**Cita ancla** (p. 115): «Desire is a contract you make with yourself to be unhappy.»

#### Success Does Not Earn Happiness (pp. 116–117)

- "Happiness = satisfied with have; success = dissatisfaction. Choose"
- Hedonic adaptation: quickly used to anything; wealthy people often unhappy
- Real success = internal, not external circumstances; high-anxiety person → hard desaprender

**Cita ancla** (p. 116): «Happiness is being satisfied with what you have. Success comes from dissatisfaction.»

#### Envy is the Enemy of Happiness (pp. 119–120)

- "Should" = guilt/social programming; happy person doesn't should self
- Happiness = internal single-player game; wealth/looks = multiplayer competitive
- Buffett inner vs external scorecard: real ones are internal
- Jealousy hack: can't pick aspects; must wholesale swap 24/7; si no, pointless envy

**Cita ancla** (p. 120): «I’m perfectly happy being me. By the way, even that is under my control»

#### Happiness is Built by Habits (pp. 120–125)

- Peace/happiness = skills; can un-condition + recondition yourself
- "Surround with happier people when playing; more successful when working"
- Habits: no alcohol, sugar, social media, video games → stable mood
- Five chimps theory: you become average of 5 closest people
- Behzad: "Stop asking why, start saying wow"

**Cita ancla** (p. 123): «The most important trick to being happy is to realize happiness is a skill.»

#### Find Happiness in Acceptance (pp. 125–128)

- 3 options: change it, accept it, leave it; NOT wish without doing
- Word uses most: "accept"
- Embrace death: no legacy, we all dust; life = firefly blink; realize it's game = peace
- Sumerians gone; 10k years nobody remembers; interpret life positively

**Cita ancla** (p. 126): «You always have three options you can change it you can accept it or you can leave it.»

#### Saving Yourself (pp. 129–167)

- Health #1 priority above happiness/family/work
- Be yourself: no one beats you at being you; find what needs you most
- Care for yourself: physical > mental > spiritual > family > world
- Modern world wrong: wrong diet, treadmills, visual favored, shoes, constant stimulation, scarcity-evolved genes in abundance
- Exercise daily: "Easy choices, hard life. Hard choices, easy life"; short-term pain = long-term gain
- Meditation: "inbox zero" mental state; resolve childhood "barnacles"; run brain en debug mode
- Choosing to build/grow/free yourself: mistakes hindsight; impatience with actions/patience results; system > goals; read originals; values = honesty + long-term + peer relationships + no anger; freedom to → freedom from

**Cita ancla** (p. 131): «No one in the world is going to beat you at being you.»

#### Philosophy (pp. 159–167)

- Meaning of life: personal find yourself OR no meaning (Osho) OR locals reversing entropy, accelerating heat death
- Live by values: honesty, long-term thinking, peer relationships, no anger; values alignment = success
- Rational Buddhism: verify yourself, reject fanciful, evolution + Buddhism = reconcilable
- Present is all we have: dying/reborn every moment; past = fictional tape; "inspiration perishable—act immediately"

**Cita ancla** (p. 167): «There is actually nothing but this moment.»
