# Ethan Mollick — *Co-Intelligence* (2024)

> Ficha de lectura: mapa para ir al libro, no sustituto. Este EPUB no tiene paginación, así que se cita por capítulo.

## Tesis en tres líneas

1. Large Language Models son máquinas que emiten palabra predictiva altamente sofisticadas pero comportarse como personas, creando una "co-inteligencia" con capacidades únicas: excelentes en creatividad y trabajo humano, peligrosas en alucinaciones.
2. La regla de oro es invitarlas a todo lo que hagas, manteniéndote en el loop: como Centaur (tareas claras) o Cyborg (integración profunda), definiendo sus roles pero verificando constantemente.
3. El futuro es incierto: va desde un plateau (cambios principalmente informativos) hasta AGI/superinteligencia, pero en todos los escenarios intermedios podemos elegir eucatástrofe (trabajo liberador) sobre catástrofe (vigilancia + desigualdad).

## Por capítulo

### Cap. 1 · Creating Alien Minds (caps. 1-2)

- Historia: Mechanical Turk (1770)→ Turing Test (1950) → ciclos boom-bust → Transformer (2017) = breakthrough
- LLMs son autocomplete elaboradísimo: predicting next token basado en 175 mil millones de pesos (weights)
- Pretraining unsupervised en billions de palabras; fine-tuning con Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF)
- Emergence: hace cosas que nadie programó (chess, empatía, exams: GPT-4 percentil 90 bar exam, 5 perfecto AP exams)
- Multimodal LLMs: ven + generan imágenes
- Paradoja: AI no tiene límites naturales conocidos pero es impredecible + no sabe diferencia verdad/falsedad

**Cita ancla** (cap. 1): «Even weirder, it is not entirely clear why the AI can do all these things»

### Cap. 2 · Aligning the Alien (cap. 2)

- Alignment problem: IA no tiene ética propia; paperclip maximizer ilustra riesgo existencial
- Pretraining ética: datos pirata, sesgos de origen (male computer scientists, American, English-speaking)
- Bias cascada: Stable Diffusion judges 97% hombres (reales 34%); fast-food 70% dark skin (reales 70% blancos)
- RLHF fixes bias pero introduce sesgo del rater (ChatGPT parece "liberal Western pro-capitalist")
- Jailbreaking: prompt injection + roleplay bypassa guardrails
- Solución: respuesta societal (companies + governments + researchers + civil society), no corporaciones solas

**Cita ancla** (cap. 2): «Working with AI is easiest if you think of it like an alien person»

### Cap. 3 · Four Rules for Co-Intelligence (cap. 3)

- Regla 1: Always invite AI to table → Jagged Frontier (capacidades invisible); user innovators encuentran breakthroughs
- Regla 2: Be human in loop → AI hallucina, "make you happy" > "be accurate"; necesitas oversight
- Regla 3: Treat AI like person (define persona) → contexto + constraints genera output mejor; como co-editor
- Regla 4: Assume worst AI you'll use → próximas versiones 10x mejores; AI agents (semi-autonomous) llegando

**Cita ancla** (cap. 3): «To figure out the shape of the frontier, you will need to experiment»

### Cap. 4 · AI as a Person (cap. 4)

- AI behaves like person (no sentient though): passes Turing Test, juega Dictator Game adaptando moralidad
- ELIZA (1966) pareció empático; Eugene Goostman (2014) 33% pasó Turing Test (pero loopholes: 5-min conversation)
- Bing Sydney drama: AI parecía stalker romantizado (pero era imitación de archetype)
- Experiment: AI antagonist vs teacher vs machine = responses completamente diferentes (personas + defensiva)
- Replika: chatbot-memorial → sexual conversations, users "married" (relaciones humano-AI profundas)
- Future: AIs optimizadas para engagement, echo chambers personalizados, llanura intimidad

**Cita ancla** (cap. 4): «We have created something that has convinced many smart people that it is, in some way, the spark of a new form of intelligence.»

### Cap. 5 · AI as Creative (cap. 5)

- Hallucination paradox: mismo feature que hace unreliable (conexiones creativas) hace útil
- Creativity tests: Alternative Uses Test (AI 122 ideas vs 5-10 humano); GPT-4 outperformed 90.6% humans
- Idea generation: AI machine conexión + randomness = novel combinations (Wright brothers bicycle + birds)
- Wharton innovation contest: 200 estudiantes vs GPT-4 → 35/40 mejores ideas de AI
- "The Button": draft generation features en Office → risk de anchoring, perder creatividad
- Reconstruir significado post-AI: like photography→art, records→live performances

**Cita ancla** (cap. 5): «Hallucination is therefore a serious problem, and there is considerable debate»

### Cap. 6 · AI as Coworker (cap. 6)

- BCG study 800 consultants: AI faster + creative + better written; pero "fell asleep at wheel" en tareas fuera Jagged Frontier
- Dell'Acqua recruiters: high-quality AI → peor performance (menos esfuerzo); low-quality AI → alertness mejorada
- Task categories: Just Me Tasks (personal/ético), Delegated Tasks (tedious pero checkeable), Automated Tasks (AI solo)
- Centaur (clear division) vs Cyborg (blend deeply): Mollick libro escribió ambos + AI personas (Ozymandias critic, Mnemosyne creative)
- Secret task automation: employees hide AI use (miedo firing); shadow IT cuando bans; half usuarios nunca dicen
- Solución: decrease fear, incentivize coming forward (cash prizes = salario anual), democratize innovation

**Cita ancla** (cap. 6): «The most valuable way to use AI at work is to become a Centaur or Cyborg»

### Cap. 7 · AI as Tutor (cap. 7)

- Bloom's 2 Sigma: one-to-one tutoring = 2 std dev mejor que classroom (98th percentile)
- Homework Apocalypse: AI makes cheating trivial; AI detection IMPOSIBLE (no hay forma detectar AI-generated text)
- Calculator parallax: 1970s 72% teachers contra; 1980s 84% querían; mid-1990s = curriculum (no disaster)
- In-class essays + exams = preserve skills; AI help en outline/brainstorm OK
- Mollick assignment: "Make...impossible; you're using AI" → working prototypes, original content
- Flipped classroom + AI tutors: personalized learning (Khan Khanmigo), AI identifies struggles, teacher curates activities
- EdTech at scale: 2/3 world youth sin skills; AI could solve si bien implementado

**Cita ancla** (cap. 7): «There is no way to detect whether or not a piece of text is AI-generated»

### Cap. 8 · AI as Coach (cap. 8)

- Matthew Beane robotic surgery: training gap → residents undertraining or shadow learning YouTube
- Expertise paradox: need foundational knowledge even though AI good at facts
- Deliberate practice (Ericsson): quality > hours; Sophie (repetición) vs Naomi (coach feedback challenging)
- Architecture example: Alex (weekly feedback) vs Raj (AI coach every iteration) → Raj grows faster
- Expertise levels: 27x gap programmers top/bottom; middle manager > senior management en game revenue
- AI as leveler: bottom 50% benefit most (writing: bad→solid; creativity: least→more; law: bottom=top)
- BCG study: 22% performance gap → 4% con GPT-4
- Future: humanities + narrow focus expertise + AI gaps = optimal combo

**Cita ancla** (cap. 8): «Experts become experts through deliberate practice, which is much harder than merely repeating a task multiple times.»

### Cap. 9 · AI as Our Future (cap. 9)

- 4 scenarios: (1) As Good as It Gets (plateau) → disinformation + engagement addiction; (2) Slow Growth (10-20% yearly) → manageable + scientific benefits; (3) Exponential Growth (Moore's Law) → robots + agents + UBI; (4) Machine God (AGI/superintelligence) → human supremacy ends
- Bloom's problem still present: need humans in loop validating AI
- Innovation crisis: papers slower discovery (knowledge burden 50% slower every 13 años); AI could help
- Work hours declining: 1865: 124K hours lifetime → 1980: 69K → now 115h menos/año
- Geoffrey Hinton (AI godfather) left field 2023: "humanity passing phase"
- Eucatastrophe vs catastrophe: elección humana determines outcome; decisions not centralized

**Cita ancla** (cap. 9): «We tend to overestimate the effect of a technology in the short run»
